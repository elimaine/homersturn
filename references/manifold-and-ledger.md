# The manifold and the ledger: the reasoning behind the skill

Drawn from [Effect Co](https://effectco.game)'s Poet (meaning layering, the ledger of readings) and
its pacing engine (2026-11-02), with the academic survey behind it.

## Why a manifold

A sentence is read, not decoded: a reader holds several readings at once and
settles them as the passage goes on. Good writing manages that cloud. It
leaves open the readings that will pay later (foreshadowing is a reading
kept open until its turn), closes the ones that would mislead, and keeps the
plain reading sound so a first reader is never lost. Poet's method: list what
else the sentence can mean, close the readings you did not mean, add a layer
only where one is wanted, cut the phrase that opens a reading nobody will
pay.

## Why a ledger

Surprisal is cost. Readers pay more than linearly for a sentence that packs
more than they expected (uniform information density: a super-linear
processing cost for spikes). So meaning must be spread, and spread where the
reader is willing to pay: where a promise has been raised and they want it
paid. The ledger is the running balance:

- **Debt**: words read since something was last paid. Past a few hundred, a
  release is due: a short line that pays what little is owed and cuts the
  stretch off.
- **Due**: the weight of promises ripe to pay: questions the reader has been
  pulling at, a planted hint now told twice, a turn whose moment has come.
- **Reveal**: a promised turn has arrived; density may rise toward it and a
  landing punch pays it.

Dense raises; a punch pays. Dense where nothing is due is noise. A punch where
nothing is owed is melodrama.

## Why rhythm is emotion

Rhythm patterns are emotional modes before they are stylistic ones. The five
used here: staccato (fear, urgency), chaos (panic, ecstasy), lyrical (grief,
love), flowing (ease), stillness (awe, aftermath). The feeling a passage
leaves picks the rhythm of the next, and a threat overrides it. Valve's AI
Director rule holds for prose: build, sustain, peak, fade, relax; do not
switch rhythm while the scene is engaged.

## Why the stress position

Gopen and Swan: readers assign weight to the end of a syntactic unit. Put the
consequential thing there; put the familiar thing at the start. A paragraph
that ends on the setting has spent its stress position on nothing, and a
dense sentence put there is pure cost.

## Why forms

Song and verse forms are grammars of return: verse-chorus, the pre-chorus as
the bridge, AABA, rondo, sonata recapitulation, the villanelle's refrain. A
refrain is density by recurrence, which costs the reader nothing because the
words are already theirs; its meaning shifts because everything between the
tellings changed. The musical sentence (state, restate, fragment and
accelerate, cadence) is the shape of a fight. Caplin's cadence is the punch.

## Why transitions

Most pacing failures are at the seams. A form held too long bores; a form
dropped mid-way jars. The bridge paragraph (a pre-chorus: momentum, no new
fact) carries the reader across; a cadence that opens the next form is an
elision and reads as inevitability.

## Ely, Frankel and Kamenica

Suspense is the expectation of a belief moving; surprise is the move. Twists
land harder when rarer, and late in a piece they should be larger and fewer.
Do not spend a late landing on a small thing.

## The measurements that catch the failures

After a paragraph, ask: what band did it land in (plain, lean, dense, punch)?
Is the last sentence the one where the most changes? Did a held question
move? What does it make the reader feel, and how keyed up is it? Which planted
line did it carry? These five answers are enough to find every dense sentence
on a low-stakes beat and every stretch that has run too long unpaid.
