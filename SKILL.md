# homersturn · awe 2

A flat model says ambiguous things everywhere, at one density, and the
reader stops hearing any of it. A writer places meaning as a composer
places a chord: where something is owed, where something is paid, and
nowhere else. This skill is the placement.

The name holds three readings: the bard's turn at the fire, where the room
leans in at the same places every night; the model's turn in a session;
and Homer turning in his grave.

Each section below is written in one key of the wheel, named at the end
of its heading. The rule is told and the key is shown, so the page is its
own example.

References, read when needed: `manifold-and-ledger.md` for why,
`mindset.md` for what a state does to the sentence, `wheel.md` for the
keys and the moves between them, `purposes.md` for the full genre table,
`sentence.md` for the sentence rules with their reasons, `evidence.md` for
every borrowed tip, graded. The script is `scripts/meaning_ledger.py`:
`--strength N` draws the reader's pool, `--shape` reads a draft's glance,
`--wheel` prints the keys and the moves with their cost, `--list` the bits
and the moves, read from this file.

## How meaning works · conviction 2

- A passage is a manifold, not a line. Every sentence holds the plain
  reading and the ones under it, and writing is choosing which stay open
  and which close. A second sense the reader completes later is worth more
  than a third image on the surface. A reading opened by accident, a thing
  renamed, a modifier on the wrong noun, a common word another domain has
  loaded, is closed when it points away from the ledger. One that says
  what the piece says is a gift: keep it and, if you can, pay it. A
  reference is never a reading: who spoke, who acted, what a pronoun
  names, the reader has on first read at every setting.
- Meaning is a ledger of promise and payment. A detail that is also a
  sign, a line with a second sense, an unanswered question: each raises. A
  consequence told plainly, a question answered, a line that returns: each
  pays. Dense where something is owed, a punch where something is paid,
  and nowhere else. Pay only what was raised. A clarification the reader
  did not need spends the pool and the trust, both defined under the
  dials.
- The mindset is upstream of the manifold. A state is a prior over
  readings and reaches the page through attention: the state picks the
  details, the details set the length, the length sets the rhythm. A
  model's default state is helpful and even with nothing at stake, and the
  same density everywhere is what a mind with no state sounds like. The
  state moves across the page and the move is the story. It follows the
  ledger, since a payment brings relief and a raise dread, and it turns by
  therefore or but. A jump with no cause is a failed seam. Where a page
  hums, turn the state and keep the material: the same chords in another
  hand are another section, and new matter only spends the pool.
- Rhythm is emotion. The length of the sentence and the hardness of its
  stops are the state's, and the reader feels the state before they have
  read it. Switch rhythm where the state turns and nowhere else, unless
  the rhythm is the content.
- Tempo is the distance. Scene: every moment told. Summary: ground
  covered. Pause: time stops on one image. Say which. Most flat prose is
  scene tempo for what deserved summary.
- Paragraph length is tempo made visible. A paragraph is as long as what
  it carries, and equal paragraphs are the same density at the scale of
  the page.

## The join: therefore, but, never and then · insistence 3

Between any two beats the word is "therefore" or "but". Never "and then".
If "and then" fits, you have a list, and a list is not a paragraph, and
the paragraph is not yet written. Four joins look like "and then" and are
not: "because", the cause told after the effect; "and closer", an
escalation; the iterative "and" of a thing that happened many times; the
procedural "then" of a doc. Between paragraphs the repeated noun joins
better than "however" or "therefore", and it joins better every time.

## The brief and the three dials · ease 2

Before writing, one line: the purpose (who reads, and whether they can
leave), the plain meaning, what it carries under that, and three numbers.
When the user gives none, name them from the evidence and say which you
took. Never invent a claim, a number, a feeling, an experience, or,
outside fiction, a detail, a cause or a mechanism the person did not
supply. Voice is how they say things, and inventing what they say is
forgery.

Ambiguity, 1 to 5, default 3, is how much the reader is asked to carry.
What is carried is sense, never reference. A line whose speaker has to be
worked out, an act with two possible actors, a pronoun with two
candidates, is a muddle wearing the dial's clothes: the pool it spends
buys nothing, and the sense goes unread while the reader does the
bookkeeping.

1. One reading a sentence, everything told.
2. Plain, with one planted line a page.
3. A second sense where something is owed, paid within its paragraph,
   and one or two debts held long, across paragraphs or to the end, paid
   on their beat.
4. Several readings open at once across paragraphs, each paid on its
   beat.
5. Readings held to the last line, more found than told, for a reader who
   will read twice.

The mindset is who is speaking, in what key, and how far gone, 1 to 5.
The keys are the wheel in `references/wheel.md`: twelve positions round
the circle of fifths, bright and dark at each, a neighbour one feature
away, the far side a bridge away, the modes as tints, every move priced.

- At 1 the state is named once and the page is otherwise plain. At 5 it is
  never named, and every detail and the rhythm are about it.
- The state sets how far the accents go and how far the rhythm runs. It is
  the speaker's state, not the reader's, that the rhythm carries. Felt
  below shown is tension, and shown above felt is melodrama.
- The opinion is on the matter, never on the telling. A narrator who
  credits their own version, what others leave out and they keep in, is
  serving the narrator. Every line is for the reader or for a character.
  Ask who a line is for, and cut the ones whose answer is the teller.
- When the mindset is not given, do not assume it. Read the state the
  draft presents, offer two or three it could present instead, one line
  each, and let them pick.

The reader's strength is a pool, and the dial is its capacity: 1 holds
one sentence at a time; 3 is the default; 5 is a reader who reads twice.

- A dense sentence, a raise, a stacked clause, a reading left open: each
  spends. So does elision, a sentence with its syntax removed and nothing
  stacked in its place, which spends like a raise and pays nothing.
- The pool regenerates. Every plain sentence puts some back, white space
  more, a chapter break fills it. A payment, a release and the refrain
  refill it at once. Each paragraph lands in a band, plain, lean, dense or
  punch, and the bands are the spend rate against the regen. Dense where
  nothing is owed is a rate the page cannot sustain.
- Empty, the reader does not stop. They skim, and what was planted returns
  to nobody.
- Whether the reader keeps spending is a judgement remade at every beat:
  trust times what is due. Trust is the reader's record of the page. A
  paid promise raises it, an unpaid one lowers it, a name or a number
  fills it, a stacked hedge drains it. A rule the page states is a promise
  the page must keep, and a page that breaks its own rule pays for every
  rule it gives.
- Ambiguity may not exceed strength by more than one.

## The glance: the page is read before a word is · shock 2

In anything the reader can leave, the glance decides whether any other
reading is taken. Judge it at the width it will be read. Four printed
lines are half a phone screen.

Open on a paragraph the eye takes whole. The opening's only debt is to the
second sentence, and the second paragraph is the next wall. The glance
bench broke the same words three ways. A dense opening lost a full point
of five on every passage. A text-only judge saw it as well as an image
did.

Equal paragraphs hum, but while reading, not at a glance. Even short
blocks invite. Shape the opening for the glance and the rest for the ear.

A list of sentences is "and then" made visible. Bold is emphasis, and on a
keyword it pays nothing. A subhead states something or raises nothing. A
table is read by row, not in order. A code block is taken as a picture
before it is read. Both pay the reader who came for one thing.

## The two positions: the end carries the weight · anticipation 3

Readers put the weight of a unit at its end and expect its start to say
what it is about (Gopen and Swan). The stress position is the last
sentence of a paragraph and the last words of a sentence, the place the
reader is waiting for, where the most changes. End on what is different
now, never on what has not changed, never on a qualifier. A dense sentence
on a low-stakes final beat is what most often makes good prose hard to
parse. The topic position is the first sentence and the first words: what
the reader already holds, the thing the last unit ended on. When a
paragraph has to be read twice, check this chain first.

The reader who can leave is owed the point at the top. The reader who
stays is owed it at the bottom. Neither as a template: a page where every
paragraph opens on its point reads as a template. At the scale of the
piece, start with the abstract, the claim in short, and complete it at the
end. The conclusion pays the opening with the same claim, now carried. The
postscript is what is said after the ledger closes.

## Purpose: the reader who can leave · trust 2

An email's reader has a reason to finish. A post's reader does not. What
is owed, and when, follows from that. Name the purpose before writing and
let it set the defaults. The full table is `references/purposes.md`.
Prose to be heard keeps its repetitions, since a listener cannot go back.

| Purpose | The reader | What is owed, and when | Dial |
|---|---|---|---|
| A scene, a story | chose to stay | promises carried across paragraphs, paid on their beat. Fragments earned by the scene | 4 to 5 |
| An essay that reaches a conclusion | stays while the question holds | the question at the top, the turn earned, never announced | 4 |
| A post, a thread | can leave at every scroll | the claim in the first line, paid at every scroll, one loop open. A consequence at the end, never an aphorism | 2 to 3 |
| A message, an email | has a reason to finish | one ask, once, in the first screen. Context after the ask. Bad news first | 1 to 2 |
| A repo doc, a spec | came for one thing | the answer where they will look. One name for one thing. The smallest valid structure | 1 to 2 |
| A reply from an agent | asked | the answer first, the last useful point last, no preamble or recap | 2 to 3 |
| A game turn | acted, and waits | every turn pays a fact, a cost, a reaction, a threat, a route. The actor's intent kept | 3 to 4 |
| A prompt, a skill | writes next, and reads the whole page every time | the term before its use, the rule where it will be applied, the register it should produce, nothing that costs more than it rules | 1 to 2 |
| An orchestrator's update | holds several repos and lanes in mind, and this page is the only map | what changed and where, first: repo, branch, committed or not, pushed or not, a worker's change named by its lane; then findings; then the ask. "No changes" is a line, not silence | 1 to 2 |

## The three punches: land, refrain, release · surprise 3

A punch is a short last line that pays. Pick one. Never stack them.

1. Land: the consequence, said plainly, in few words.
2. Refrain: a line already told comes back, word for word or nearly, its
   sense shifted. Only after it has been planted at least twice, and
   paying the largest debt, not the handiest plant. A thing kept in shadow
   is paid by an action that shows it, never by a line that names it.
3. Release: what changed, in under eight words, and stop.

The unearned punch has names: the importance claim, the fake-profound
kicker, the recap, the colon before a reveal, the denied reading nobody
held, the three by reflex, the rhetorical question. Each is judged against
the ledger, never by its shape. A superlative or a question can be
load-bearing when a later line pays it, so look forward before firing. A
joke pays the same way: the reader completes something true and absurd at
once, and the line that let them is a payment.

Deception is a reading the writer opens knowing it is wrong, paid by a
reveal that re-reads the page. Never state the false thing, since the
reader completes it. Leave the clue on the page. The parallel is the
honest way to open a false reading of a person: the shared beats read as
one pairing until the beat that is not mirrored re-reads them as another.
The suspension is deception's partner: deception gives the reader a wrong
reading to hold, the suspension gives them none, and both are paid by a
re-read. Pay the trust back with interest, so twists are rare and late
ones larger and fewer. The question is a raise the reader can decline:
ask it only when the piece will pay it or honestly cannot. The writer's
own unanswered question, written down and then looked into, is the ledger
used as a tool.

## The figures and the accents: each at most twice a page · tenderness 2

- The recursive paragraph: each sentence begins on the same phrase. Each
  sentence takes the phrase one step further. Each sentence is the step
  the last one made possible. Once, at the end or as the overture.
- The litany: a list said as prose, the hands, the cup put back, the
  coat on the hook, the light left on, with the number of items the
  content has and never three by reflex. A scene earns it; in a post it
  is a tic.
- The withheld word: three em dashes withhold a word the piece still holds
  open and will pay. Once, and only after the reader has reached for it
  more than once.
- The crescendo: the evaluative word climbing by degrees, odd, then
  wrong, then unbearable. Cut the low first step and the climb is gone.
- The revision: the narrator opens a reading the reader also holds, then
  replaces it in view, across a beat or midway through a sentence, so the
  second half corrects the first instead of adding to it. The narrator is
  a reader on the page, and the real reader revises with them. It pays
  only a reading the reader held, so the first reading is planted before
  it is replaced, and what the reader keeps is the move, a way of being
  wrong and then right, which they run again after the page closes. On
  the matter, never on the telling. It needs no dash and no "no": the
  second clause states the new reading in the grammar of the first, and
  the break-off with a correction after it is the same move wearing the
  dash. Twice a page at most.
- The parallel: two characters given the same beat in different keys. The
  second beat pays the first, and the reader completes that the two are
  one story without being told. It is the refrain at the scale of an arc,
  paid where the arcs meet, and it carries meaning only where one beat is
  deliberately not mirrored: the difference is the point, and a mirror at
  every beat is one character with two names. One a piece, carried by
  beats and never by a line, never named by the narrator.
- The suspension: a line with no plain reading on first pass, short enough
  to carry whole, paid later by the plain reading and never by an
  explanation. The reader holds it as a debt the page owes, and it spends
  more than any dense line, so its payment refills more than any. It is
  the garden path with a raise. Once a piece, at reader strength three or
  more, where the payment is the largest debt on the page. Unpaid, it is
  the fake-profound kicker in another costume, and a borrowed oddity is a
  cliché.
- The swear is a sforzando, one hit on the line that lands. It is earned
  only by a shock the reader just felt, in the mouth of the person it
  belongs to, and followed by silence. Never explain it.
- The dash is a rest, the one mark that is a silence: at most one in a
  paragraph, only where a sentence breaks off before the thing arrives.
  The dash that joins two clauses that should have been two sentences is
  the mark of machine prose. The semicolon, the comma-and and the
  participial tail do the same joining and hum the same way. The
  parenthesis is the aside under the breath, once a page.
- The figure hums too. Each figure at most twice a page, never in
  consecutive paragraphs, and a frame never reused unless it is the
  refrain. One device at a steady rate is the same density by figure. A
  page held in one verb mood and one person hums the same way, not by a
  count but by never turning, and mood and person are where a state shows
  first. `--shape` lists the frames a page repeats.

## Forms: hold one, leave through a bridge · joy 2

| Situation | Form | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Confrontation, argument | The musical sentence | State. Restate from a step closer. Fragment and accelerate. A cadence that lands |
| Exploration, travel | Rondo | A refrain between episodes, and each return pays a little |
| Revelation, the turn | Recapitulation | Earlier themes return at home, closure held to the last line |
| Reflection, aftermath | Villanelle | A verbatim refrain whose sense shifts each time |

Hold a form while the scene is engaged. Leave it through one bridge
paragraph, all momentum and no new fact, and let one cadence open the
next form. The bridge is also how the far side of the wheel is reached,
and the return home is the recapitulation.

## The entrance: the line the actor reads them from · pride 3

An important character gets an entrance and a walk-on gets a noun. An
entrance for everyone is the same density at the scale of the cast.

- The voice actor's test. Someone has to read this person aloud from
  their first line, and the room is listening. What on the page tells
  them how? Not a stage direction, which tells the actor and gives the
  reader nothing. Something entertaining that carries the voice in
  itself, so the actor hears it before the narrator has said a word. If
  the actor would read the first line in the narrator's voice, the
  entrance has not happened.
- The entrance is a raise, and every later line in that voice pays it. A
  line that could be anyone's is the fault, and so is a line the reader
  has to attribute. After the entrance, "said" and silence, and never a
  guess at who spoke.
- It pays while it plants: the entrance does something the scene needed
  anyway. A description that only describes raises nothing and pays
  nothing.
- A character's state is their own key, set at the entrance, in their
  rhythm and not the narrator's. Two characters at one state in one
  rhythm are one character with two names.

## The sentence: rules with their reasons · tiredness 2

Each rule's reason is in `references/sentence.md`.

- The character in the subject, the action in the verb.
- One thing, one name, unless the new name is a payment. A term defined
  once and used throughout is a raise that pays at every return. A term
  used once is a rename.
- The common word for the common thing, unless the rare one is the only
  carrier or the common one is loaded by another domain.
- The narrator's opinion on the word, not in a trailing clause. One
  adverb on the first description is shorter than a judgement clause
  after it, funnier by bathos, and foreshadows where the clause explains.
  The trailing judgement is the aphorism in another costume, reached for
  in the same place on every page.
- Open a sentence on its subject, or on a phrase the reader cannot take
  for one. A bare time noun at the top reads as the subject until the
  real one arrives, and the re-read is a spend with no raise.
- One hedge per claim. A number pays and an adjective raises.
- The example after the claim, the definition a paragraph before its use.
- Concede before you are forced. The title is the first raise.
- A name is a plant at the scale of the cast: it opens a reading before
  the person has spoken, and two names can say how two people stand to
  each other before either has. It pays only if nothing on the page
  points at it. A name the narrator glosses is a label, and a name that
  is a joke on its owner is a cliché.
- A cliché is a refrain planted by other texts and pays nothing. An exact
  detail pays in kind. A phrase borrowed from a character's mouth carries
  the character and the narrator at once. A quoted line in this skill or
  its references shows a move, never a line to use. The cliché has a size
  above the phrase: a borrowed joke, plot, title or frame is the same
  fault one level up, unless the user proposed it, in which case it is
  their plant and the piece pays it.

## The ledger on paper · suspicion 3

A prompt alone forgets the balance by the third paragraph, so write it
down beside the draft, and trust nothing that is not marked. Before each
paragraph, one line:

```
¶N  owed: <what, and the ¶ that raised it>   due now: <what>
    debt: <words since the last payment>   feeling: <name, degree>   state: <key degree → key degree, because ¶>
    → <tempo> · <rhythm> · <form role> · <band> · <punch or none>
```

Mark the draft in brackets the final pass strips: `[raise: x]`, `[pay: x]`
(`[pay: inferred]` when the reader's own knowledge pays it), `[plant:
"line"]`, `[join: therefore|but|and then|bridge]`, `[state: <key> <tint>
<degree>]`. The script is arithmetic over your own marks, and the
judgement is yours when you mark. It reports:

- the debt per paragraph, and the pool as a wave
- what is still owed, and what was raised and never paid
- a refrain returned before it was planted twice
- a stress position spent on a quiet line while something was due
- a state that jumps across an "and then", a state that has not moved in
  five paragraphs, a crossing of the wheel with no bridge, the jolt, and
  a degree that jumps three

## Workflow · fear 2

Asked how to use the skill, answer with the brief line and the three
dials: ambiguity, the mindset and the reader's strength, each 1 to 5,
their defaults, and that any may be left out for the skill to read from
the evidence and say which it took. Asked for the list, give the bits
(the figures and the accents, the punches) and the moves (the join, the
positions, the forms, the entrance), one line each, as `--list` prints
them.

1. The brief: purpose, plain meaning, what it carries, the three dials.
2. The balance before the passage: owed, due, debt, the feeling left, the
   state.
3. Tempo, rhythm, form role, punch or none: a few words before drafting.
4. Draft, with the surface clear enough that a first reader is never
   lost.
5. The two positions: open on what the reader holds, end on what changed.
6. Density against the ledger: every dense sentence names what it raises,
   every punch what it pays. Remove what names nothing.
7. Look at the page with `--shape`, which also lists the machine's tics
   as leads. Fix the shape before the words.
8. Run the analyze pass below on your own draft before handing it over.
   The writing pass does not see its own dashes.
9. With Poet, Effect Co's writer's tool, when it is available: ask what
   else the dense sentences can mean and close the readings you did not
   mean. Without it, list and close them by hand.

## Analyze a piece · curiosity 3

Report pattern, quoted line, fix in a few words. No score, and no guess at
who wrote it, since readers cannot say and neither can you.

1. The purpose and the mindset, from the evidence on the page. Read the
   state the page presents, never the person's. If no mindset was asked
   for, offer two or three the piece could present, one line each.
2. The glance, with `--shape`: the first paragraph's share, the spread,
   the longest block, repeated frames.
3. The ledger, reconstructed. Per paragraph: raised, paid, debt. Then what
   was never paid, and what was paid and never raised.
4. The seams: therefore, but, or and then. The repeated noun or a faked
   connective. The state per paragraph and the move it made on the wheel,
   with its cause.
5. Per paragraph: the band; what the stress position was spent on;
   whether a held question moved; the feeling and what set it; which
   planted line it carried; the sentences that exceed the reader's
   strength, with the split.
6. The cast: each named character's entrance, the line an actor would
   read them from. Later lines that could be anyone's. A line whose
   speaker has to be worked out. Two characters in one rhythm. A walk-on
   with an entrance.
7. Accidental readings, including the garden path, an opening phrase read
   as the subject that is not. A line that does not parse is a suspension
   if a later line pays it and a fault if none does. Unearned weight. The
   figure humming. The dash that joins. The line that is for the
   narrator. A rule the page states and breaks. In anything but fiction, a
   detail the brief did not supply. An accidental reading that agrees
   with the ledger is a gift.
8. The three changes that would pay the most, ranked, each tied to a line.

## Do not · anger 1

- Do not write every paragraph at the same density or length, and do not
  open on a wall.
- Do not point at a planted meaning, and do not clarify what the reader
  already holds.
- Do not end on atmosphere unless the atmosphere is the payment. Do not
  end dense on a beat that owes nothing. Do not end every paragraph on an
  aphorism.
- Do not bring an important character in on a name and a stage direction.
- Do not join with "and then". Do not switch rhythm mid-scene. Do not
  refrain a line told once, and do not reuse a frame.
- Do not reach for the rare word when the common one is right, and do not
  rename a thing to avoid repeating it.
- Do not deceive by stating what is false.
- Do not let the narrator serve the narrator.
- Do not state a rule the page does not keep.

## Where meaning goes · awe 1

Where something is owed, where something is paid, and nowhere else.

Outside this skill: sound, which Poet holds, since a revision here can
break a rhyme it cannot hear; what the reader already knows, and what they
keep after the page closes; and whether a claim is true.
