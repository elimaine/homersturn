# homersturn · awe 3

The room leans in at the same places every night, and has for three
thousand years. A writer places meaning as a composer places a chord:
where something is owed, where something is paid, and nowhere else.
Spread at one density everywhere, it goes unheard.

This skill is the placement.

The name holds three readings. The bard's turn at the fire. The model's
turn in a session. Homer turning in his grave.

Each section below is written in one key of the wheel, named at the end
of its heading. The rule is told and the key is shown, so the page is its
own example. Most sit at degree 1 to 3, since a reference reads at piano;
one is at 5, and it is the shortest. A few rules are broken on the page,
each where the break shows the rule better than keeping it would.

References, read when needed: `manifold-and-ledger.md` for why,
`mindset.md` for what a state does to the sentence, `wheel.md` for the
keys and the moves between them, `purposes.md` for the full genre table,
`sentence.md` for the sentence rules with their reasons, `evidence.md` for
every borrowed tip, graded. The script is `scripts/meaning_ledger.py`:
`--strength N` draws the reader's pool, `--shape` reads a draft's glance,
`--wheel` prints the keys and the moves with their cost, `--list` the bits
and the moves, read from this file.

## How meaning works · conviction 3

- A passage is a manifold. Every sentence holds its plain reading and
  the ones under it, and writing is choosing which stay open and which
  close. The strongest objection is that a second sense is the writer's
  private pleasure, and the reader walks past it into an empty room. It
  is true of a second sense put anywhere. It is not true of one put
  where the reader is already waiting, and that placement is this
  skill. The name of the skill holds three readings and a reader takes
  all three in a line. A second sense the reader completes later is
  worth more than a third image on the surface. A reading opened by
  accident, a thing renamed, a modifier on the wrong noun, a common word
  another domain has loaded, is closed when it points away from the
  ledger. One that says what the piece says is a gift: keep it and pay
  it. Who is meant is never a reading: who spoke, who acted, what a
  pronoun names, the reader has on first read at every setting.
- Meaning is a ledger of promise and payment. A detail that is also a
  sign, a line with a second sense, an unanswered question: each raises. A
  consequence told plainly, a question answered, a line that returns: each
  pays. Dense where something is owed, a punch where something is paid,
  and nowhere else. Pay only what was raised. A clarification the reader
  already holds spends the pool and the trust, both defined under the
  dials.
- The mindset is upstream of the manifold. A state is a prior over
  readings and reaches the page through attention: the state picks the
  details, the details set the length, the length sets the rhythm. A
  model's default state is helpful and even, with the stakes left out,
  and the same density everywhere is the sound of a mind in no key. The
  state moves across the page and the move is the story. It follows the
  ledger, since a payment brings relief and a raise dread, and it turns by
  therefore or but. A jump carries its cause, or the seam shows. Where a page
  hums, turn the state and keep the material: the same chords in another
  hand are another section, and new matter only spends the pool.
- Rhythm is emotion. The length of the sentence and the hardness of its
  stops are the state's, and the reader feels the state before they have
  read it. Switch rhythm where the state turns and nowhere else, unless
  the rhythm is the content.
- Tempo is the distance. Scene: every moment told. Summary: ground
  covered. Pause: time stops on one image. Say which. Most flat prose is
  scene tempo for what deserved summary.
- Paragraph length is tempo made visible. A paragraph is as long as what
  it carries, and equal paragraphs are the same density at the scale of
  the page.

## The join: therefore, but, never and then · insistence 3

Between any two beats the word is "therefore" or "but". Never "and then".
Where "and then" fits, you have a list, and a list is a list, and the
paragraph is still to be written. Four joins look like "and then" and are
honest: "because",
the cause told after the effect; "and closer", an
escalation; the iterative "and" of a thing that happened many times; the
procedural "then" of a doc. Between paragraphs the repeated noun joins
better than "however" or "therefore", and it does not wear.

## The brief and the three dials · ease 2

Before writing, one line: the purpose (who reads, and whether they can
leave), the plain meaning, what it carries under that, and three numbers.
When the user leaves them out, name them from the evidence and say which
you took. Never invent a claim, a number, a feeling, an experience, or,
outside fiction, a detail, a cause or a mechanism the person left out.
Voice is how they say things, and inventing what they say is forgery.

Ambiguity, 1 to 5, default 3, is how much the reader is asked to carry.
What is carried is sense, and who is meant stays fixed. A line whose speaker has to be
worked out, an act with two possible actors, a pronoun with two
candidates, is a muddle wearing the dial's clothes: the pool it spends
buys nothing, and the sense goes unread while the reader does the
bookkeeping.

1. One reading a sentence, everything told.
2. Plain, with one planted line a page.
3. A second sense where something is owed, paid within its paragraph,
   and one or two debts held long, across paragraphs or to the end, paid
   on their beat.
4. Several readings open at once across paragraphs, each paid on its
   beat.
5. Readings held to the last line, more found than told, for a reader who
   will read twice.

The mindset is who is speaking, in what key, and how far gone, 1 to 5.
The keys are the wheel in `references/wheel.md`: twelve positions round
the circle of fifths, bright and dark at each, a neighbour one feature
away, the far side a bridge away, the modes as tints, every move priced.

- At 1 the state is named once and the page is otherwise plain. At 5 it
  goes unnamed, and every detail and the rhythm are about it.
- The state sets how far the accents go and how far the rhythm runs. The
  rhythm carries the speaker's state, and the reader's feeling follows.
  Felt below shown is tension, and shown above felt is melodrama.
- The opinion is on the matter, never on the telling. A narrator who
  credits their own version, what others leave out and they keep in, is
  serving the narrator. Every line is for the reader or for a character.
  Ask who a line is for, and cut the ones whose answer is the teller.
- When the mindset is left out, read the state the draft presents, offer
  two or three it could present instead, one line each, and let them
  pick.

The reader's strength is a pool, and the dial is its capacity: 1 holds
one sentence at a time; 3 is the default; 5 is a reader who reads twice.

- A dense sentence, a raise, a stacked clause, a reading left open: each
  spends. So does elision, a sentence with its syntax removed and the
  place left empty, which spends like a raise and pays as one.
- The pool regenerates. Every plain sentence puts some back, white space
  more, a chapter break fills it. A payment, a release and the refrain
  refill it at once. Each paragraph lands in a band, plain, lean, dense or
  punch, and the bands are the spend rate against the regen. Dense while
  the ledger is empty is a rate the page runs out under.
- Empty, the reader skims on, and what was planted returns to an empty
  room.
- Whether the reader keeps spending is a judgement remade at every beat:
  trust times what is due. Trust is the reader's record of the page. A
  paid promise raises it, an unpaid one lowers it, a name or a number
  fills it, a stacked hedge drains it. A rule the page states is a promise
  the page must keep, and a page that breaks its own rule unpaid pays for
  every rule it gives.
- Ambiguity stays within one of strength.

## The glance: the page is read before a word is · shock 2

In anything the reader can leave, the glance decides whether a word is
read. Judge it at the width it will be read. Four printed
lines are half a phone screen.

Open on a paragraph the eye takes whole. The opening's only debt is to the
second sentence, and the second paragraph is the next wall. The glance
bench set the same words three ways. A dense opening lost a full point
of five on every passage. A text-only judge saw it as well as an image
did.

Equal paragraphs invite at a glance, since even short blocks invite,
and hum once read. Shape the opening for the glance and the rest for
the ear.

A list of sentences is "and then" made visible. Bold is emphasis, spent
on the keyword it sits on. A subhead states something. A table is read by
row, in the reader's order. A code block is taken as a picture before it
is read. The table and the code block pay the reader who came for one
thing.

## The hook: the first raise · curiosity 2

The hook is the first raise, made where the glance lands: a promise of
the whole that the reader stays to see paid. What it promises is the
genre's. A post hooks on the claim, when the claim surprises. An essay
hooks on the tension, ahead of the thesis. A story hooks on the one thing
already wrong in an ordinary room, or the one thing already right. A
message hooks on the reason to read, in the first line. An epic hooks
on its subject in its first line, in the middle of things, with the
room already listening. The title is the
hook's first word. A hook the piece pays late pays with interest, and a
hook the piece leaves unpaid is the importance claim in its best clothes.

A question is a raise the reader can decline. The one the page answers,
now or a paragraph on, is honest; the one the reader answers with a
planted line is a payment they make, and with a line they already held
it is the clarification wearing a question mark; the one held to the end
is the hook, which draws hardest when the answer is half in view and
costs trust when it never comes. Ask only what the piece will pay or
honestly cannot.

## The two positions: the end carries the weight · anticipation 3

Readers put the weight of a unit at its end and expect its start to say
what it is about (Gopen and Swan). The stress position is the last
sentence of a paragraph and the last words of a sentence, the place the
reader is waiting for, where the most changes. End on what is different
now, never on what has not changed, never on a qualifier. A dense sentence
on a low-stakes final beat is what most often makes good prose hard to
parse. The topic position is the first sentence and the first words: what
the reader already holds, the thing the last unit ended on. When a
paragraph has to be read twice, check this chain first.

The reader who can leave is owed the point at the top. The reader who
stays is owed it at the bottom. Neither as a template: a page where every
paragraph opens on its point reads as a template. At the scale of the
piece, start with the abstract, the claim in short, and complete it at the
end. The conclusion pays the opening with the same claim, now carried. The
postscript is what is said after the ledger closes.

## Purpose: the reader who can leave · trust 2

An email's reader has a reason to finish. A post's reader can leave at any line. What
is owed, and when, follows from that, and so does the tint at the two
ends: the mood the hook is made in, which is what draws the reader, and
the mood the piece is owed to end in. The ledger owns the moves between.
Name the purpose before writing and let it set the defaults. The full
table is `references/purposes.md`. Prose to be heard keeps its
repetitions, since a listener cannot go back.

## The three punches: land, refrain, release · surprise 4

A punch is a short last line that pays. One. Not two.

1. Land: the consequence, said plainly, in few words.
2. Refrain: a line already told comes back, word for word or nearly, and
   its sense has turned. Planted twice first, never once. It pays the
   largest debt on the page, not the handiest plant. A thing kept in
   shadow is paid by an act that shows it. Name it and the shadow is
   spent.
3. Release: what changed, in under eight words. Stop.

One punch, and it can pay many. Several debts paid by one plain act is
the largest punch there is, the recapitulation in a line: the refill is
the sum of what each debt spent, and the act stays one and short. Plant
toward it. The one thing it cannot do is pay what was never raised.

The punch position can raise instead of pay. A short plain line at the
end that opens the largest debt is the cut, and it is the largest raise
there is, since the reader carries it at the weight of a payment. It is
not random: it is about the matter, and the page owes it sooner than
anything else it has raised. Unpaid, it was the fake-profound kicker.

The unearned punch has seven names. The importance claim. The
fake-profound kicker. The recap. The colon before a reveal. The denied
reading nobody held. The three by reflex. The rhetorical question. Judge
each against the ledger and not its shape: a superlative or a question
is a raise when a later line pays it, so look forward before firing. A joke pays the same way. The reader completes something true
and absurd at once, and the line that let them is a payment.

Deception is a reading the writer opens knowing it is wrong, paid by a
reveal that re-reads the page. The reader completes the false thing, so
the page leaves it unsaid and the clue in view. The parallel is the
honest way to open a false reading of a person: two people share a beat
and read as one pairing, until the unmirrored beat re-reads them as
another. The suspension is deception's partner. Deception hands the
reader a wrong reading to hold. The suspension hands a line whose
reading is still to come. A re-read pays both. Pay the trust back with
interest, so twists are rare and the late ones larger and fewer. The writer's own unanswered question,
written down and then looked into, is the ledger used as a tool.

## The joke: a payment the reader makes · ease 3

A joke is the ledger run fast. The setup plants, the pause is a stress
position with nothing in it, and the punch pays with a line that re-reads
the setup, so the reader completes something true and absurd at once,
and the completing is the laugh. What makes it a joke and not a jolt is
that the violation is harmless and the reader holds both readings at
the same moment; surprise on its own does not do it. The funniest word
goes last, since anything after it is the recap. The pause runs longer
the bigger the turn, and on the page the pause is the paragraph break.
The deadpan is felt above shown, the violation told at ease's volume. A
joke costs: it lifts attention and liking and lowers what the reader
thinks of the teller's judgement, so an essay spends one where the claim
can afford it and a message spends none. We laugh at the mechanical
where the living should be, which is the same density everywhere, so a
page that hums is a joke the machine does not know it is telling.
Explained, a joke is dead. Why?

## The figures and the accents: each at most twice a page · tenderness 2

- The recursive paragraph: each sentence begins on the same phrase. Each
  sentence takes the phrase one step further. Each sentence is the step
  the last one made possible. Once, at the end or as the overture.
- The litany: a list said as prose, the hands, the cup put back, the
  coat on the hook, the light left on, with the number of items the
  content has, three only when the content has three. A scene earns it;
  in a post it is a tic.
- The withheld word: three em dashes withhold a word the piece still holds
  open and will pay. Once, and only after the reader has reached for it
  more than once.
- The crescendo: the evaluative word climbing by degrees, odd, then
  wrong, then unbearable. Cut the low first step and the climb is gone.
- The revision: the narrator opens a reading the reader also holds, then
  replaces it in view, across a beat or midway through a sentence, so the
  second half corrects the first instead of adding to it. The narrator is
  a reader on the page, and the real reader revises with them. It pays
  only a reading the reader held, so the first reading is planted before
  it is replaced, and what the reader keeps is the move, a way of being
  wrong and then right, which they run again after the page closes. It
  is on the matter. It runs without a dash and without a "no": the second
  clause states the new reading in the grammar of the first, and
  the break-off with a correction after it is the same move wearing the
  dash. Twice a page at most.
- The parallel: two characters given the same beat in different keys. The
  second beat pays the first, and the reader completes that the two are
  one story without being told. It is the refrain at the scale of an arc,
  paid where the arcs meet, and it carries meaning only where one beat is
  left unmirrored on purpose: the difference is the point, and a mirror
  at every beat is one character with two names. One a piece, carried by
  beats, with the narrator silent about it.
- The suspension: a line whose plain reading arrives later, short enough
  to carry whole, paid by that plain reading when it comes and spoiled by
  an explanation. The reader holds it as a debt the page owes, and it spends
  more than any dense line, so its payment refills more than any. It is
  the garden path with a raise. Once a piece, at reader strength three or
  more, where the payment is the largest debt on the page. Unpaid, it is
  the fake-profound kicker in another costume, and a borrowed oddity is a
  cliché.
- The swear is a sforzando, one hit on the line that lands. It is earned
  only by a shock the reader just felt, in the mouth of the person it
  belongs to, and followed by silence, which is the whole of its
  explanation.
- The dash is a rest, the one mark that is a silence: at most one in a
  paragraph, only where a sentence breaks off before the —
  The dash that joins two clauses that should have been two sentences is
  the mark of machine prose. The semicolon, the comma-and and the
  participial tail do the same joining and hum the same way. The
  parenthesis is the aside under the breath, once a page.
- The figure hums too. Each figure at most twice a page, with a paragraph
  between, and a frame reused only as the refrain. One device at a steady
  rate is the same density by figure. A page held in one verb mood and
  one person hums the same way, by holding rather than by count, and mood
  and person are where a state shows first. `--shape` lists the frames a page repeats.

## Forms: hold one, leave through a bridge · joy 2

| Situation | Form | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Confrontation, argument | The musical sentence | State. Restate from a step closer. Fragment and accelerate. A cadence that lands |
| Exploration, travel | Rondo | A refrain between episodes, and each return pays a little |
| Revelation, the turn | Recapitulation | Earlier themes return at home, closure held to the last line |
| Reflection, aftermath | Villanelle | A verbatim refrain whose sense shifts each time |

Hold a form while the scene holds. Leave it through one bridge
paragraph, all momentum and no new fact, and let one cadence open the
next form. The bridge is also how the far side of the wheel is reached,
and the return home is the recapitulation.

## The entrance: the line the actor reads them from · pride 3

An important character gets an entrance and a walk-on gets a noun. An
entrance for everyone is the same density at the scale of the cast.

- The voice actor's test. Someone has to read this person aloud from
  their first line, and the room is listening. What on the page tells
  them how? A stage direction tells the actor alone. Something
  entertaining carries the voice in itself, so the actor hears it before
  the narrator has said a word. An actor who would read the first line
  in the narrator's voice has met a walk-on.
- The entrance is a raise, and every later line in that voice pays it. A
  line that could be anyone's is the fault, and so is a line the reader
  has to attribute. After the entrance, "said" and silence, with who
  spoke on the page.
- It pays while it plants: the entrance does something the scene needed
  anyway. A description that only describes sits outside the ledger.
- A character's state is their own key, set at the entrance, in their
  own rhythm, apart from the narrator's. Two characters at one state in one
  rhythm are one character with two names.

## The sentence: rules with their reasons · tiredness 2

Each rule's reason is in `references/sentence.md`.

- The doer in the subject, the action in the verb.
- One thing, one name, unless the new name is a payment. A term defined
  once and used throughout is a raise that pays at every return. A term
  used once is a rename.
- The common word for the common thing, unless the rare one is the only
  carrier or the common one is loaded by another domain.
- The narrator's opinion in the word itself. One
  adverb on the first description is shorter than a judgement clause
  after it, funnier by bathos, and foreshadows where the clause explains.
  The trailing judgement is the aphorism in another costume, reached for
  in the same place on every page.
- Open a sentence on its subject, or on a phrase the reader cannot take
  for one. A bare time noun at the top reads as the subject until the
  real one arrives, and the re-read is a spend with no raise.
- One hedge per claim, on the number and never on the claim. A number
  pays and an adjective raises.
- The example after the claim, the definition a paragraph before its use.
- Concede before you are forced. The title is the first raise.
- A name is a plant at the scale of the cast: it opens a reading before
  the person has spoken, and two names can say how two people stand to
  each other before either has. It pays while the page leaves it
  unpointed at. A name the narrator glosses is a label, and a name that
  is a joke on its owner is a cliché.
- A cliché is a refrain planted by other texts, and its payment went to
  them. An exact detail pays in kind. A phrase borrowed from a
  character's mouth carries the character and the narrator at once. A
  quoted line in this skill or its references shows a move, and the line
  stays here. The cliché has a size
  above the phrase: a borrowed joke, plot, title or frame is the same
  fault one level up, unless the user proposed it, in which case it is
  their plant and the piece pays it.

## The ledger on paper · suspicion 3

A prompt alone forgets the balance by the third paragraph, so write it
down beside the draft, and trust the marks alone. Before each
paragraph, one line:

```
¶N  owed: <what, and the ¶ that raised it>   due now: <what>
    carried: <words since the last payment>   feeling: <name, degree>   state: <key degree → key degree, because ¶>
    → <tempo> · <rhythm> · <form role> · <band> · <punch or none>
```

Mark the draft in brackets the final pass strips: `[raise: x]`, `[pay: x]`
(`[pay: inferred]` when the reader's own knowledge pays it), `[plant:
"line"]`, `[join: therefore|but|and then|bridge]`, `[state: <key> <tint>
<degree>]`. The script is arithmetic over your own marks, and the
judgement is yours when you mark. It reports:

- the words carried per paragraph, and the pool as a wave
- what is still owed, and what was raised and is still unpaid
- a refrain returned before it was planted twice
- a stress position spent on a quiet line while something was due
- a state that jumps across an "and then", a state that has not moved in
  five paragraphs, a crossing of the wheel with no bridge, the jolt, and
  a degree that jumps three

## Workflow · fear 2

Asked how to use the skill, answer with the brief line and the three
dials: ambiguity, the mindset and the reader's strength, each 1 to 5,
their defaults, and that any may be left out for the skill to read from
the evidence and say which it took. Asked for the list, give the bits
(the figures and the accents, the punches) and the moves (the join, the
positions, the forms, the entrance), one line each, as `--list` prints
them.

1. The brief: purpose, plain meaning, what it carries, the three dials.
2. The balance before the passage: owed, due, carried, the feeling left, the
   state.
3. Tempo, rhythm, form role, punch or none: a few words before drafting.
4. Draft, with the surface clear enough that a first reader always has
   the plain reading.
5. The two positions: open on what the reader holds, end on what changed.
6. Density against the ledger: every dense sentence names what it raises,
   every punch what it pays. Cut what names neither.
7. Look at the page with `--shape`, which also lists the machine's tics
   as leads. Fix the shape before the words.
8. Run the analyze pass below on your own draft before handing it over.
   The writing pass is blind to its own dashes.
9. With Poet, Effect Co's writer's tool, when it is available: ask what
   else the dense sentences can mean and close the readings outside your
   intent. Without it, list and close them by hand.

## Analyze a piece · curiosity 3

The writer who asked will read the first thing on the page and act on
it, so the three changes that would pay the most come first, ranked,
each tied to a quoted line with the fix in a few words. What follows is
the evidence they rest on, and it is read second if at all. Pattern,
quoted line, fix. The score and the guess at authorship are left off,
since readers cannot say and neither can you. Name what fails and why;
the piece is already on the page, so do not retell it, and a ledger
line is a raise and a payment, not the paragraph in other words.

1. The purpose and the mindset, from the evidence on the page. Read the
   state the page presents rather than the person's. Where the mindset
   was left open, offer two or three the piece could present, one line
   each. The tint at the two ends against the purpose's column: the
   draw the hook makes and the mood the end is owed.
2. The glance, with `--shape`: the first paragraph's share, the spread,
   the longest block, repeated frames. A heavy first paragraph that is
   the draw, dark at the top and lifted after, is the tint column
   kept, and the glance rule bends for it in the genres whose reader
   chose to stay.
3. The ledger, reconstructed, one line per paragraph: what was raised,
   what was paid, what is carried. Then what stayed unpaid, and what was
   paid without a raise.
4. The seams: therefore, but, or and then. The repeated noun or a faked
   connective. The state per paragraph and the move it made on the wheel,
   with its cause.
5. Per paragraph, only where something is wrong: the band; what the
   stress position was spent on; whether a held question moved; the
   feeling and what set it; which planted line it carried; the
   sentences that exceed the reader's strength, with the split. A
   paragraph with nothing wrong gets no line.
6. The cast: each named character's entrance, the line an actor would
   read them from. Later lines that could be anyone's. A line whose
   speaker has to be worked out. Two characters in one rhythm. A walk-on
   with an entrance.
7. Accidental readings, including the garden path, an opening phrase read
   as the subject in the subject's place. A line that holds its parse
   back is a suspension when a later line pays it and a fault otherwise.
   Unearned weight. The figure humming. The dash that joins. The line
   that is for the narrator. A rule the page states and breaks without paying. In
   anything but fiction, a detail from outside the brief. An accidental
   reading that agrees with the ledger is a gift.

## Breaking a rule · relish 3

Every rule above is a promise the page made, and a reader who has taken
the rules tastes the break before the sentence ends. That is its use.
Rules kept everywhere are the same density by rule, and the reader stops
hearing them as they stop hearing a steady beat. One broken, in view,
wakes them: a line that steps off the norm is read slower and rated
stronger, by readers who know no theory. And a page that breaks a rule
and pays it has shown it knew the rule, which earns more trust than
keeping it would, the way a spilled cup helps the candidate who was
already winning and sinks the one who was not. So the break is a raise,
and it costs most at strength one. It reads as a mistake until it is
paid, and it is paid when the break is the content: the sentence that
runs past the reader's strength because the speaker cannot stop, the
"and then" of a mind too tired to join, the wall at the top that is the
draw, the aphorism at the end that is the land, the dash where the thing
does not arrive. This page is a skill, and its own table keys a skill
low and level; it is keyed round the whole wheel and up to five,
because the keys are what it teaches, and that is the break paid by its
content. One rule at a time, late, after the page has kept it in view,
where the reader can see which and can tell it was chosen, and paid
within reach, mostly.

## Do not · anger 5

Fortissimo, once on the page, so it is short.

- No wall at the top. No page where every paragraph weighs the same.
- Never point at the planted meaning. Never explain what the reader
  already holds.
- No ending on atmosphere that pays nothing. No dense close on a beat
  that owes nothing. No aphorism on every paragraph.
- No important character brought in on a name and a stage direction.
- No "and then". No rhythm switched mid-scene. No refrain of a line
  told once. No frame used twice but as the refrain.
- No rare word where the common one is right. No renaming a thing to
  dodge repeating it.
- Never deceive by stating what is false.
- No narrator serving the narrator.
- No rule stated that the page does not keep. This page keeps every
  fucking one of its own.

## Where meaning goes · awe 1

Where something is owed, where something is paid, and nowhere else.

Outside this skill: sound, which Poet holds, since a revision here can
break a rhyme it cannot hear; what the reader already knows, and what they
keep after the page closes; and whether a claim is true.
