## Piece

The tap was the loudest thing in the house. He had lain awake since one counting the gap between drops, nine, nine, ten, and at two, on the tenth ten, he was on his feet.

The wrench was in the drawer under the tea towels, where it had always been. He shut the stopcock under the sink, opened the tap to let it drain, and took the handle off. The washer came out black and flat, worn to a disc. You just change the washer, she had said, the last time, with her hand on his wrist to stop him forcing it. He had not changed it then either.

He found the spare in the jar with the fuses and seated it, screwed the handle down, opened the stopcock. He did not force it. Water came, ran clean. He shut it off. The spout held one drop, swelling.

It fell. He stood with the wrench and waited for the next one. Nine. Ten. Twenty. The fridge clicked on, and he counted through its hum, past where he could call it the tap, and did not go back to bed. The tap held. He kept counting.
