## Piece

The man who owned the bakery in 1986 handed Ruth the back-door key at ten and went home to bed. The ovens at two, he said, the loaves at four, and if she got frightened, that was just the quiet. It went when the fans came on. She was twenty-two. She stood in the dark kitchen with the key in her fist and listened to the compressor, and at two she lit the ovens, and at four the fans came on, and it went.

Tonight she handed the same key to Mae, who had it on her own ring before Ruth finished talking.

"Two, ovens. Four, loaves. I wrote it on my hand." Mae held up the hand. "Go home. You said you'd go home."

"If you get frightened—"

"That's just the quiet. I know."

Ruth went. The back door shut behind her and the lock turned, and Mae was alone with the compressor and the clock. She did not get frightened. She got cold, and then busy, and at two she lit the ovens, and at four the loaves went in and the fans came on, and the first smell came up through the flour like somebody arriving.

Across the street, Ruth had been sitting in the car since ten with the engine off. At four the window went yellow. At ten past, the smell came through the vent. She waited until she was sure of it, bread and not anything else, and then she was sure, and there was nothing left to be sure of, and the car was cold. That was just the quiet. She started the engine so the fans would come on.
