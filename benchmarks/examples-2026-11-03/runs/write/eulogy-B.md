## Ledger

Brief: a eulogy, read aloud at a graveside; listeners stay and cannot go back, so repetitions kept. Plain: Ferris lived nineteen years and slept on the writer's chest every night. Under it: the weight is gone and tonight the writer lies down without it; the second sense of *weight*, who was holding whom, is the one debt. Nothing supplied beyond the years and the chest, so no colour, no cause, no last day. Ambiguity 3, mindset: grief, held plain, 3, strength 3.

Ferris lived nineteen years, and he slept on my chest every night of them. [plant: "every night of them"] Every night. [plant 2] I have gone looking for one he missed and I cannot find it. [join: but] I used to think he came for the warmth. He came for the weight [revision: warmth → weight; raise: weight, whose], his on me so I would know he was there, and mine under him so he would know I had not left. [join: therefore] Nineteen years is near seven thousand nights of being held down [pay: number; raise carried: held down], and I never once wanted up. [join: but; state 3 → 4] Tonight I will lie down at the usual hour, and my chest will wait at the usual place, and that is all I have to say about tonight. [join: therefore; state 4 → 3] The ground will be on him now. He never minded weight, and I hope it holds him the way he held me. [pay: weight, who held whom] He slept on my chest every night of them. [refrain, sense shifted: past tense carries the whole nineteen years, and the ground]

Closed readings: "held down" could read as pinned or trapped; "never once wanted up" closes it. "That is all I have to say about tonight" could read as the speaker refusing the room; it is for the room, a told limit, so kept. No dash, no stacked punch: the refrain is the one hit, and "good night" was cut because it would be a second.

## Piece

Ferris lived nineteen years, and he slept on my chest every night of them. Every night. I have gone looking for one he missed and I cannot find it. I used to think he came for the warmth. He came for the weight, his on me so I would know he was there, and mine under him so he would know I had not left. Nineteen years is near seven thousand nights of being held down, and I never once wanted up. Tonight I will lie down at the usual hour, and my chest will wait at the usual place, and that is all I have to say about tonight. The ground will be on him now. He never minded weight, and I hope it holds him the way he held me. He slept on my chest every night of them.
