## Piece

He had turned the tap off twice and it dripped. He stood at the sink in the dark and counted between drops. Four. Four. Three, then four again. The clock over the door said ten past two, and had said ten past two for a long time.

The spanner was under the sink where it had always been. The washers were in a jam jar with the lid written on, and he turned the jar so he could not read the lid. He shut the stopcock. He undid the head, lifted out the old washer and held it to the window: split clean across, worn thin at the rim. Years of that. He pressed a new one in and it went home with a small sound he liked.

He tightened the head down, opened the stopcock and ran the tap, hard, then turned it off. Nothing. He waited for the drip the way you wait for a step on the stair that does not come. Nothing. He put the spanner away and the jar back, lid to the wall.

The kitchen was very quiet with nothing to count. The fridge ran and stopped. The clock said ten past two. He went back to the sink and turned the tap on, just a thread, and left it.
