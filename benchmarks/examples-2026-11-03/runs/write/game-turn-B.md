## Ledger

Brief: game turn; the reader acted and waits, and can't leave until the turn pays. Plain: the lock gives, the cellar is there with the ledger in it, the innkeeper's sleep holds and then goes quiet. Under: the ledger is someone else's (I took "stolen" as stolen *by* the innkeeper, since it's in his cellar) and the house has one sound that is the player's cover. Dials taken: ambiguity 3, mindset: narrator at the player's ear, held breath, 3 → 4, strength 3. Form: rondo in miniature, the snore as refrain. Owed by the genre: a fact, a cost, a reaction, a threat, a route, the intent kept (they picked the lock; they did not say they'd go down).

```
¶1  owed: the lock (the player's action)   due now: does it open, and at what cost
    debt: 0   feeling: concentration, 2   state: held breath 3, because the action
    → scene · short stops, one per pin · opening cadence · plain · none

¶2  owed: nothing   due now: the fact (ledger), the route (steps), the plant for the threat
    debt: 0   feeling: curiosity, 3   state: held breath 3 → 3, because ¶1 paid clean
    → scene, one pause on the ledger · longer, falling · episode · lean · none

¶3  owed: the snore, ¶2   due now: the reaction
    debt: 112   feeling: dread, 4   state: held breath 3 → 4, because the refrain breaks
    → pause · two stops · refrain return · punch · land

¶4  owed: the threat, ¶3; the route; the intent   due now: the route, the player's hand back on the choice
    debt: 0   feeling: dread held, 4   state: 4, not moved, because nothing has followed yet
    → scene · short, plain · closing cadence · plain · release
```

## Piece

The lock is older than the door. Two pins give at a touch. The third keeps the tip of your pick, and the half you have left is enough. The hasp lifts. The door leans in a hand's width on its own weight and you catch it before it finishes.

Cold comes up the steps and the smell of ale gone into stone. Six steps, a lantern hook with no lantern on it, barrels down the left wall. The ledger sits where a man keeps a thing he likes to look at, on the nearest barrel, under a cloth that has taken its shape. The name tooled into the cover is not the innkeeper's. Over your head, through two floors of boards, the innkeeper snores. You have been hearing it since the yard, which is to say you have stopped hearing it.

It stops. You hear that.

Nothing follows. No boards, no feet. The ledger is six steps down. The door is open in your hand.
