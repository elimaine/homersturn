**Analyze pass.** The glance: a two-word opening, then three blocks that shorten; nothing to fix. Readings opened by accident: "the sensible thing closes" reads as Dana not being sensible, which agrees with the ledger and is kept. "She isn't there" could read as death; closed by "the next time it snows" and by the room, who know where she is. No dash, no semicolon, one parenthesis-shaped aside ("which is how the best librarians teach") and it carries the turn, so it stays. The refrain "open" returns after two plants, paying the largest debt, and nothing names it. Every "and" in ¶2 is iterative. The three in "Schools close. Roads close. The sensible thing closes" is a crescendo by bathos, not three by reflex, and the joke is the step.

## Piece

Thirty years.

Thirty years is a lot of winters, and every one of them snowed on Dana. Most things close for snow. Schools close. Roads close. The sensible thing closes. The library did not. Not once. Not one morning in thirty years did anyone in this town walk up to that door and find it locked against the weather. It was open. It was always open.

So we learned something, without being taught it, which is how the best librarians teach. We learned that there is a place in this town you can count on, and a person inside it, and that the weather is not a reason. I will tell you how deep that went. The next time it snows, half of us will have our boots on before we remember she isn't there.

Dana, the door was yours. Thirty years, and it was open. To Dana.
