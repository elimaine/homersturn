The river wakes slowly, its surface a sheet of pewter beneath the lifting mist. Fog curls off the water in soft, drifting ribbons, snagging on the reeds along the banks before dissolving into the pale gold light spreading across the eastern sky. For a long moment the world holds its breath. Then the first birds begin, a scattered chorus of wrens and warblers threading through the willows, their calls sharp and bright in the cool air.

Along the shore, the stones are slick with dew. Each blade of grass bends under its own glittering bead of water, and the mud at the edges bears the delicate tracks of a heron who has already come and gone. Where the current bends sharply around a fallen log, the water darkens to the color of strong tea, swirling and folding over itself with a low, steady murmur that seems older than memory.

Slowly, the light deepens. The mist lifts from the middle of the channel, revealing the river's true color: a shifting blend of green, bronze, and reflected sky. Ripples catch the sunlight and scatter it into a thousand moving sparks. Somewhere upstream, a fish breaks the surface, and the circles it leaves spread outward until they meet the bank and vanish.

The air smells of wet earth, cold stone, and the faint sweetness of river mint. A breeze stirs the cattails, and they sway in unison like patient sentinels. Across the water, the far bank steps out of shadow, its trees shedding the last of the night's dimness, their leaves catching fire with color.

By the time the sun clears the hills, the river is fully awake, flowing onward with the easy confidence of something that has done this every morning for a thousand years and will do it again tomorrow.
