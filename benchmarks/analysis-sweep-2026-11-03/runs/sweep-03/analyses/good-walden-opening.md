# Analysis

The `--shape` script was not run here. The counts below are by hand.

## 1. Purpose and mindset

**Purpose: the opening of a book-length, first-person essay.** I took the row "An essay that reaches a conclusion," at dial 4. The evidence:

- It refers to itself as "the following pages" and "this book," and speaks to "my readers."
- It sets two tenses: past for the woods, present for now.
- There is no dialogue and no scene, so it is not a story.
- There are no subheads and no claim in line one, so it is not a post.

The rules that turn with this genre:

- The reader stays, so withholding is allowed. Why the writer went to the woods is never said here.
- Several loops may stay open.
- The point may come at the bottom.
- The litany belongs to a scene, so the opening sentence borrows a figure from another genre.

The table has no row for a preface. This opening also behaves like a paper's abstract: "I undertake to answer some of these questions in this book" states what the book will pay.

**Mindset: composed, dry, needled by the town's curiosity. How far gone: 2.**

- Sentences run long through semicolons, then break short at assertions: "I lived there two years and two months."
- What is named: place, duration, labor, the neighbors' questions.
- What is walked around: why he went, what it was like, any feeling at all.
- No accents are spent. There is no swear and no dash. The irritation surfaces only as irony: "very natural and pertinent."
- The hedges are partly mock: "Unfortunately," "Perhaps," "more particularly."

The writer did not say which mindset to present, so this is the help mode. Here is the line "I should not talk so much about myself if there were anybody else whom I knew as well," rewritten in three states:

- **Earnest, 1:** "I write about myself because mine is the only life I know well enough to report truly."
- **Openly irritated, 3:** "You asked, so I will talk about myself; I know no one else well enough to talk about."
- **Defiant, 4:** "I will talk about myself. I know no one better."

It ends on the adverb that carries the claim: "by the labor of my hands only." The glance flags it; the read clears it.

## 4. The seams

The only paragraph break: "a sojourner in civilized life again" turns to "I should not obtrude my affairs."

- **Join: but.** He is back among the townspeople, but he is about to talk about himself, which wants an excuse.
- **Link:** "civilized life" returns as "my mode of life." It is a repeated noun, but it sits at word 30, not in the topic slot.

Inside ¶2 are the seams that should have been paragraph breaks:

- **S4 to S5:** "in this book" turns to "In most books." The repeated noun is honest. Join: but.
- **S8 to S9:** "Moreover" is a connective faking motion. The content turns from apology ("the narrowness of my experience") to demand ("I require of every writer"). That is a *but* labelled as an *and*.
- **S9 to S10 and S11 to S12:** therefore. If every writer owes a sincere account, then these pages are one, so the next question is whom they are for.

## 6. Topic position

- **The read-twice sentence is S1:** "concerning my mode of life, which some would call impertinent, though they do not appear to me at all impertinent."
  - "Which" first attaches to "mode of life," the nearest noun. Then the plural "they" forces "inquiries," and the reader backs up.
  - The opening adds a counterfactual double negative: "I should not obtrude…if very particular inquiries had not been made."
- **The known-new chain elsewhere is strong:**
  - "this book" → "In most books" → "in this"
  - "first person" → "the first person that is speaking"
  - "about myself" → "this theme"

## 7. Accidental readings

- **Pronoun with two candidates:** "my mode of life, which some would call impertinent." Fix: name the noun, as in "inquiries some would call impertinent." This double may be chosen, since his life is what some would call impertinent. If so, it is the one reading worth keeping, and the price is the reparse.
- **Negation scope:** "I should not obtrude…if very particular inquiries had not been made." Fix: say it positively, starting "Since my townsmen have asked…"
- **Rare word with no second sense:** "obtrude." It is register only. Fix: "push," or leave it as voice.
- **Rare idiom:** "first or last." It can snag on "first person" two sentences earlier, a brief stumble. Fix: "sooner or later."
- **Thing renamed:** "the following pages," "this book," "these pages." It is mild, since no reader takes them for different things. Accept.
- None found: no pointing participle and no noun stack.

## 8. Unearned weight

There is little.

- **Connective faking motion:** "Moreover, I, on my side, require of every writer." Fix: cut "Moreover" and start a new paragraph.
- **Two hedges on one claim:** "Perhaps these pages are more particularly addressed to poor students." Fix: keep one.
- **Checked and earned:**
  - The denied reading "not merely what he has heard of other men's lives": readers of most books hold it.
  - "though they do not appear to me at all impertinent": "some would call" names who holds that reading.
  - The apparent three-by-reflex ("what I got to eat; if I did not feel lonesome; if I was not afraid; and the like"): "and the like" breaks the three, and the charity questions make it five.
- **None present:** no importance claim, recap, colon reveal, dash or swear.

## 9. The three changes that would pay the most

1. **Break the wall.** The second paragraph carries four movements in one block. Break it at "In most books," "I, on my side, require," and "Perhaps these pages." Each break has an honest join (but, but, therefore), and the last movement becomes a short landing. The cost: the unbroken torrent of address goes. See the last item of the next section.
2. **Turn "Moreover" into a break.** "Moreover, I, on my side, require of every writer…" files the piece's hardest turn as an addition. Cut "Moreover" and open a paragraph on "I, on my side." This puts the demand in a topic slot and lets "a distant land to me" take the stress position of a whole paragraph.
3. **Untangle S1.** "…my mode of life, which some would call impertinent, though they…" is the topic sentence of the wall, and it must be read twice. Fix: "My townsmen have made very particular inquiries concerning my mode of life. Some would call them impertinent; to me they seem very natural and pertinent." This loses the reading where "impertinent" clings to his life.

## What the framework does not account for

**1. The same word turned at close range.**
- **The lines:** "which some would call impertinent, though they do not appear to me at all impertinent, but…very natural and pertinent," and "from a distant land; for if he has lived sincerely, it must have been in a distant land to me."
- **What it does:** the reader hears one word switch sides inside a sentence. The first use sets the ear, so the second lands as the jab.
- **The gap:** the skill's refrain needs distance and two plantings, so "do not refrain a line that was told once" would wrongly flag "distant land." Its one-name rule covers synonyms but has no name for the same word given a new sense.
- **In the model?** Yes. It is a manifold on one word, raised and paid inside a sentence, and could be added as a figure beside the litany.

**2. Irony: the plain reading as a decoy.**
- **The lines:** "Unfortunately, I am confined to this theme by the narrowness of my experience," and "how many poor children I maintained."
- **What it does:** the reader takes the apology, then hears the boast or the swipe. The humor is in the delay.
- **The gap:** the checklist would misread both. It would see "Unfortunately" as a propping adverb and "natural and pertinent" as a filler doublet. The model tells a writer to close readings or keep them open, but has no case where the surface reading exists to be rejected. It also has no check for what tips the reader; here it is the overshoot of "very natural and pertinent." Amusement is missing from the rhythm list of feelings too.
- **In the model?** Yes, with a section added. The manifold explains it, since the true reading sits under the plain one.

**3. The text sorts its reader and releases the rest.**
- **The lines:** "I will therefore ask those of my readers who feel no particular interest in me to pardon me," then "Perhaps these pages are more particularly addressed to poor students. As for the rest of my readers…"
- **What it does:** the piece tells the leaving reader they may leave and tells the staying reader who they are. Whoever stays has now chosen, which raises their stake.
- **The gap:** the skill treats "can the reader leave" as a fact of genre, set before writing, not a move inside the text. The coat line finishes the move by handing the reader a role: try it on, don't stretch the seams. None of the three punches covers a close that casts the reader.
- **In the model?** Yes. "What the reader is owed" can absorb it as a renegotiation of the debt. The purpose table also needs a row for the opening of a long work, where the reader is still deciding and the text behaves as abstract and voice at once.

**4. A rare word that carries the meaning.**
- **The line:** "At present I am a sojourner in civilized life again."
- **What it does:** "sojourner" makes civilized life the temporary, foreign stay, reversing what the reader assumed was home. "Living" or "visitor" would lose it.
- **The gap:** the skill says a second meaning falls off a rare word. Here the second meaning lives only in the rare word.
- **In the model?** Yes. The manifold explains why it works; the rule needs a carve-out.
- **Outside the model:** the period register ("obtrude," "first or last," "kindred") slows a modern reader without raising or paying anything. The skill assumes a reader contemporary with the writer.

**5. The unbroken block as stance.**
- **The evidence:** ¶2's 318 words, from "I should not obtrude" to "him whom it fits."
- **What it does:** it reads as one breath of address, answering the whole town at once without giving the floor.
- **The gap:** the skill reads paragraph length only as tempo and glance, so it can only call this block a wall.
- **In the model?** Only by extension. The mindset dial already says rhythm follows the speaker's state. Extending that to "the page's shape can carry the speaker's state" would let the skill weigh this effect against change 1 instead of just marking the block.
