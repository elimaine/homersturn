## 1. Purpose and mindset

**Genre: an agent's reply, or a reference explainer.** I took this from the evidence. There is no greeting, heading, code or dialogue, and line one answers a "how do bees…" question with no preamble. There is one in-text citation and a steady present tense describing a process. The two genres share their rules: dial 2 to 3, the answer first, the last useful point last, no recap, at most one open loop, nothing withheld. A fragment or litany would be a tic here.

The piece obeys the main rule. The point is at the top for a reader who can leave.

**Mindset: calm, informed, mildly admiring. Barely present, 1 of 5.**

- Sentences run 17 to 33 words, with one break at 8.
- Nothing is walked around, and no accents are spent.
- There are eight hedges (usually, roughly, about, ideally around, may, tend to, helps, often), all of the honest, scientific kind.
- The only feeling on the page is "remarkable," said twice.

No mindset was given, so here is the opening line in three states to pick from by ear:

- **Neutral reference, 1:** "A honeybee swarm chooses its new nest site by a vote among its scouts, and no bee is in charge."
- **Wonder, 3:** "No bee decides where the swarm will live. The swarm decides anyway, and it usually decides well."
- **Dry teacher, 2:** "The swarm has no leader for this decision; it has scouts, dances and a threshold."

## 3. The ledger, reconstructed

```
¶1  raises: "remarkable" (importance), how collective decision works,
            "led by scout bees" (led how?), "usually" (when not?),
            "suitable cavities" (what counts?), "the old queen" (so a new one?)
    pays:   who leaves, and when
    debt at end: ~20 words since a payment; the main debt (how they decide) open
¶2  pays:   "suitable" (four criteria); raises the waggle dance and pays it
            in the same paragraph (quality, place)
    raises: what happens when scouts dance for different sites
¶3  pays:   the collective part (recruitment, fading)
    s3 pays nothing new (restates s2)
    owed:   how it ends
¶4  pays:   the ending (quorum, liftoff); "collective" (no single bee in charge);
            "remarkable" with "remarkably good" (an adjective paid with an adjective)
```

- **Raised and never paid:** "usually," "led," the old queen, "enough" (quorum size), and "a height above the ground." That height is the only criterion with neither a value nor a reason, while its siblings get one.
- **Paid and never raised:** "competition." It is a new name in the last sentence for dancing that was never called that.

The piece is under a page, so the script isn't needed.

## 4. The seams

- **¶1→¶2: therefore.** "suitable cavities" → "Scouts evaluate potential sites." The link is nearly a repeated noun, broken by the rename from cavities to sites. Fix: "sites" in both.
- **¶2→¶3: therefore.** Other scouts can visit because the dance said where. But "may then" fakes a sequence where the link is causal, and "advertised" renames the dance. Fix: "Other scouts fly to the site and…"
- **¶3→¶4: and then.** "This process continues until a quorum is reached." "This process" is a label standing in for a noun. Fix: open on the thing ¶3 ended on, or turn with "but."

Tempo and rhythm:

- **Tempo** is summary throughout. ¶4 s2 (liftoff) is the one event told as an event, and then the piece steps back.
- **Rhythm** is even, medium sentences, which reads as ease and suits an explainer. The only short sentence ("This process continues until a quorum is reached") is spent on a hinge, not a landing.

## 6. The topic position

- **Known-new break:** "…usually led by scout bees. When a colony outgrows its hive…" The first sentence ends on scouts, and the second jumps back to the colony. Scouts don't return until s3. This is acceptable as abstract-then-background, but the reader holds "scouts" for a sentence with nothing to put it on.
- **Abstraction in the subject:** "The intensity and duration of her dance reflect her assessment…" The character is the scout. Fix: "How long she keeps dancing tells how good the site is…"
- **Character demoted:** "Researchers such as Thomas Seeley have shown that this decentralized method…" The subject slot goes to the researchers, and the swarm drops into an object clause. Fix: attribution after the finding, or in a clause.

## 7. Accidental readings

- **A leader opened, then denied:** "usually led by scout bees" against the closing "without any single bee being in charge." Fix: "carried out by scouts," or cut it.
- **Thing renamed:** cavities, sites, location, options, choice, home. Fix: "site" throughout, and "home" only at the landing.
- **Colony and swarm:** "Honeybee colonies choose new nest sites." The swarm, half the colony, does the choosing. Fix: "A honeybee swarm chooses."
- **Two lengths:** "duration of her dance" and "length of the waggle run" read as the same measure. Fix: "how long she keeps dancing" and "each waggle run."
- **Pronoun with candidates:** "indicate where it lies." "It" could be the site, its quality or the waggle run. Fix: "where the site is."
- **Scope:** "dance for them as well." As well as the first scout, or as well as their own site? Fix: "join the dance."
- **Label for a noun:** "This process." Fix: name it ("The dancing goes on until…").
- **Term before its definition:** "quorum." It is defined only by the next sentence. Fix: definition first, or leave it, since the genre tolerates this at sentence range.
- **Pointing clause:** "which helps prevent the swarm from getting stuck." This is the trailing participle's cousin. Fix: cut it.

## 8. Unearned weight

- **Importance claim:** "a remarkable process." Fix: cut.
- **Importance claim:** "several key features." Fix: cut "key."
- **Importance claim:** "remarkably good choices." Fix: put the finding from the source here. A number pays.
- **Recap:** "this decentralized method, which relies on competition, feedback, and a threshold of agreement." It re-labels ¶3 and ¶4. Fix: trim to the finding. The three items do map to real content, so it is not three by reflex.
- **Restating sentence:** "Scouts also tend to abandon sites that are poor" restates "weaker options fade." Fix: cut.
- **Double hedge:** "tend to… helps prevent." Fix: one hedge.
- **Hedge plus intensifier cancelling:** "often produces remarkably good." Fix: keep "often," lose "remarkably."

There is no colon reveal (the colon introduces a real list), no denied reading, no dash, no swear and no fake-profound kicker.

## 9. The three changes that would pay the most

1. **Open on the claim the end completes.** Replace "a remarkable process of collective decision-making, usually led by scout bees" with "A honeybee swarm chooses its new home with no bee in charge." Then let the last sentence complete that abstract with Seeley's finding instead of "remarkably good." This closes the leader reading and makes the piece-scale refrain earned.
2. **One name per thing.** Use "site" for cavities, location, options and choice, and "swarm" once the colony splits. Separate "how long she keeps dancing" from "each waggle run."
3. **Cut "Scouts also tend to abandon sites that are poor…" and mend the and-then seam.** End ¶3 on the fading, and open ¶4 on its noun ("The dancing ends when…"). This also breaks the four-by-three hum, since ¶3 becomes two sentences.

## What the framework does not account for

**Whether the payments are true. This is outside the model.** "Once enough scouts have visited and agreed on a single site, the swarm rapidly lifts off" pays the ledger's debt cleanly, and in the skill's terms it is a good stress-position payment. But in Seeley's account the quorum is sensed at the site: scouts there register how many others are present at once. It is not agreement among dancers at the swarm. The swarm also warms its flight muscles before it lifts off, so "rapidly" compresses a real step. Check this against the source.

A reader who knows feels the explanation go soft at its climax. A reader who doesn't is paid in counterfeit. The skill's "do not invent" governs the writer's own claims, not the accuracy of a piece under analysis. The ledger is arithmetic over placement: a false payment and a true one score the same. A section could ask "is the payment true," but nothing in the model of where meaning goes would generate that question.

**The borrowed civic frame. This works, and is inside the model.** The vocabulary runs "advertised," "support," "momentum," "agree," "quorum," "in charge." It lays a democracy over insect behavior, and it is the piece's real second sense even at dial 2. The human reader completes it as a lesson about group decisions, and the last clause ("without any single bee being in charge") pays it. It also smuggles agency in ("if they agree"). The manifold model explains this exactly: a reading kept open across the piece and paid at the end. But no section names an extended frame, or warns that its words close readings the science would leave open. A section on "the borrowed frame" would fit.

**Trust signals. These work, and could be added under what the reader is owed.** "She assesses it" quietly carries a fact (scouts are female workers). The citation "Researchers such as Thomas Seeley have shown" turns "good choices" from the writer's adjective into a finding. The parenthetical units and reasons, "(ideally around 40 liters)" and "(which is easier to defend)," pay inside the sentence that raises them. None of these raise a promise. They deposit credibility, so the reader accepts the next claim cheaper. The ledger has only raise and pay columns. A trust column, filled by citations, precise numbers and correct technical grammar and drawn down by every hedge and adjective, would let the model explain why this plain piece reads as reliable.

**Sequence as content. Partly accounted for.** The skill flags ¶3→¶4 as "and then," and the label "This process" is a fault. But in a how-it-works explainer, the timeline is what the reader came for, and "until a quorum is reached" is causal underneath (support accumulates, therefore the threshold is crossed). The join rule, taken from story beats, doesn't say that in a procedure "then" is often the therefore in plain clothes. That is a one-line genre turn the model could absorb.
