# Analysis: "Dear Sam"

I counted the shape by hand; the script was not run. The piece is under a page, so I reconstructed the ledger on paper without the script.

## 1. Purpose and mindset

**Purpose: a message.** The evidence is a greeting, a sign-off, a second-person reader, and one ask ("catch up over coffee"). The table puts a message at dial 1 to 2. Its rules are:
- One ask, once, in the first screen.
- Bad news first, the cause in one line, then what happens next.
- End on the next action.

The letter gets the bad news first: the apology is in sentence one. It breaks the other two rules:
- The cause arrives sixty words late.
- The ask sits in ¶4, about 200 words down, past the first phone screen.
- The letter ends on a promise and a recap, not the next action.

It runs at dial 1, with everything told, except one line that carries a second sense (see ¶2). That is exactly dial 2, "plain, with one planted line on a page", with the planted line in the wrong slot.

**Mindset, as read: composed contrition, 1 of 5.**
- The loss is named in line one.
- Every sentence is complete and balanced.
- No sentence breaks or fragments.
- No accent is spent.
- Every paragraph carries a hedge: "more than I can easily say", "even if it is simply", "If you are willing", "whatever works best for you".
- The register is formal throughout ("With warm regards and sincere apologies").

The state reads as regret held at arm's length. No mindset was given, so this is the help mode. Here are three states the piece could present instead, each as the opening line rewritten:

- **Ashamed, 3:** "I'm sorry. I'm the one who went quiet, and I don't have a good reason."
- **Missing them, 2:** "Sam, I've missed you, and I'm sorry it took me this long to say it."
- **Plain and direct, 1, without the formality:** "I owe you an apology. I stopped reaching out, and that was on me."

Pick by ear. I won't pick for you.

## 2. The glance

| ¶ | Words | Sentences |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 49 | 3 |
| 2 | 84 | 3 |
| 3 | 62 | 3 |
| 4 | 44 | 2 |
| 5 | 28 | 2 |

Plus the salutation and a two-line sign-off.

- **First paragraph's share:** 18% of 267 body words. That is three sentences, but about seven lines at phone width, more than half a screen.
- **Spread:** 28 to 84. The paragraphs are not equal; they thin toward the end, which is the right direction.
- **Longest block:** ¶2. The second paragraph is the next wall, about thirteen phone lines of memory before the reader learns what the letter wants.
- **Repeated openings:** three of five paragraphs open on "I", and so do five of thirteen sentences.
- **Formatting:** no lists, bold or subheads, correctly for a letter.

**What the page says unread:** a proper, conventional letter of even weight. Nothing marks the one line that matters, and the ask is invisible.

**Delete-the-first-paragraph test:** it fails, because ¶1 holds the apology. But its first clause, "I have been meaning to write to you for a long time," can go, and nothing is lost.

## 3. The ledger, reconstructed

```
¶1  raises: the apology; why the silence ("Somewhere along the way")
    pays:   the apology ("and I am sorry for it")
    owed at end: the cause
¶2  raises: you mattered (the memories); planted: "later kept getting pushed back"
    pays:   the cause ("busyness and neglect"), ~90 words after it was raised
    owed at end: what Alex wants
¶3  raises: "make things right" (in what form?)
    pays:   Sam's side of it ("I understand if you have moved on")
¶4  pays:   "make things right" with the ask
    owed at end: nothing
¶5  raises: "a better friend this time" (never paid)
    pays:   "Thank you for your patience" (never raised; contradicts ¶3)
```

- **Raised and never paid:** "I promise to be a better friend this time." The letter never says what better looks like.
- **Paid and never raised:** "patience." Sam was never asked to be patient, and ¶3 says "I do not expect you to have waited for me."

## 4. The seams

- **¶1→¶2: and then.** The letter goes from sorry to remembering. The memories are offered as context, not as the reason for the apology. No noun links the two.
- **¶2→¶3: but.** It turns from Alex's regret to Sam's side. The link is implicit ("later" becomes "waited").
- **¶3→¶4: therefore.** It is linked by the repeated verb: "to hear how your life has been" leads to "so much I want to hear". This is the best seam.
- **¶4→¶5: and then.** This is a closing appended after the ask, which already did the work.

## 5. The five measurements

| ¶ | Band | Stress position | Held question moved? | Feeling, how keyed | Planted line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | lean | "and I am sorry for it": the apology, rightly placed | opened (why), not moved | contrition, low | "silence" (returns ¶3) |
| 2 | dense where nothing is owed | "more than I can easily say": a qualifier | yes, cause paid | nostalgia, then regret, low to mid | "later kept getting pushed back", the only second sense in the letter |
| 3 | lean | "to share some of mine": the want, modest | raises "make things right" | humility, low | "silence" returns as a repeated noun; "waited" (later contradicted) |
| 4 | plain | "since we last spoke": the past, a quiet beat | pays the ask | warmth, low | none |
| 5 | plain | "a better friend this time": an abstract promise | nothing moves | formal closure, flat | "sincere" echoes ¶1 in the sign-off |

**Rhythm.** Seven of thirteen sentences join their clauses with ", and I":
- ¶1 three times ("a long time, and I owe", "into silence, and I let", "not yours, and I am sorry")
- ¶2 twice
- ¶3 and ¶5 once each

These are the moving, joined sentences the skill assigns to ease. Contrition carried at the rhythm of ease reads as nothing having cost the writer.
- **Fix:** break one of them, ideally the apology, into two sentences.

## 6. The topic position

No paragraph has to be read twice. One subject slot hides the actor: "our conversations faded into silence". The conversations do the fading until "I let years slip by" takes the blame back half a sentence later.
- **Fix:** open on the actor: "I let our conversations fade."

¶3's "Friendships take effort from both sides" puts a maxim in the subject slot, softening "my failing, not yours" from ¶1.
- **Fix:** cut it, or keep "I understand if you have moved on" alone.

## 7. Accidental readings

- **Template line contradicting the letter:** "Thank you for your patience". It implies Sam waited, after ¶3 said Alex does not expect it. Fix: cut it.
- **"This time" opens a prior attempt:** "I promise to be a better friend this time". Fix: "from now on", or cut.
- **The same verb twice, for two things:** "let years slip by" and "allowed that constancy to slip away". Fix: keep one "slip".
- **A renamed thing in adjacent clauses:** "You were a constant… that constancy". Fix: "and I let that slip" (after cutting the first "slip").
- **The rarer word:** "the adventures you have had". "Adventures" presumes and decorates. Fix: "what you've been up to".
- **Pointing at the planted meaning:** "a sincere apology" and "sincere apologies". Telling the reader it is sincere is the one thing that makes them check. Fix: cut both.

## 8. Unearned weight

- **Three by reflex (three times):**
  - "the long drives with the windows down, the late-night talks that never seemed to end, and the way you always knew how to make me laugh". Fix: one memory.
  - "coffee, a phone call, or whatever works best for you". Fix: one offer.
  - "your family, your work, and the adventures". Fix: one thing, or none.
- **The importance claim:** "more than I can easily say". It claims a weight and pays a hedge. Fix: cut it.
- **The recap:** ¶5 entire. It restates the apology, the friendship and the hope. Fix: cut it, or reduce it to one line.
- **The denied reading, earned:** "That was my failing, not yours". Sam may well hold the reading that Sam was at fault, so closing it is "concede before you are forced to". Keep it.
- **Absent, correctly:** no dash, no colon reveal, no swear.

## 9. The three changes that would pay the most

1. **Replace the stock memories with one only Alex has, supplied by Alex.** The line is "the long drives with the windows down, the late-night talks that never seemed to end". This is the three by reflex, and also a claim that Sam mattered paid with details any friend could receive. One real memory pays it. Ask Alex for the memory; do not invent one.
2. **End on the ask, as a question, and cut ¶5.** The ask "If you are willing, I would love to catch up over coffee" is a wish with nothing to answer. Make it "Would you get coffee with me?" and make it the last line. This also removes "Thank you for your patience," which contradicts ¶3, and the unpaid "better friend this time."
3. **Put "later" in ¶2's stress position.** End on "I told myself there would always be time later, but later kept getting pushed back." Cut "and I regret that more than I can easily say." The repeated "later" is the only line in the letter where a word changes meaning while being read, and it is currently followed by a hedge.

## What the framework does not account for

- **Register owed to a known reader.** "With warm regards and sincere apologies" and "Thank you for your patience" are business register, sent to someone the letter says Alex took windows-down drives with.
  - **What it does:** the reader feels the distance in the form before the content, and reads the formality as the estrangement continuing.
  - **What the skill has:** the mindset dial covers the speaker's state, the purpose table covers when things are owed, and register appears only for the swear ("the setting would not say it"). Nothing names the voice owed to a particular relationship.
  - **Within the model if added:** the relationship sets the register, and a mismatch is an accidental reading, the reader opening "we are strangers now" without the writer choosing it.

- **The specific detail as proof.** The skill has "a number pays; an adjective raises", but the stock memories fail without a weak adjective in them. They are noun phrases that are interchangeable.
  - **What it does:** in a letter to someone who was there, a detail is a test the reader runs against their own memory. A detail that could be anyone's fails the test, and the sincerity claim fails with it.
  - **Within the model if added:** a claim like "you mattered" is a raise, and only a detail particular to this reader pays it. The "do not invent" rule already says the fix must be a question back to the writer.

- **The ", and I" joiner.** The skill names the dash as machine prose's clause-joiner. Here the same job is done seven times by comma-and with no dash in sight, so the dash rule passes a letter that has the problem the rule exists for.
  - **Within the model:** this is Parker and Stone's "and then" one scale down, inside the sentence. The join rule, or the dash section, could say so.

- **The table row misfiles the reader.** The table says a message's reader "has a reason to finish". An estranged friend owes nothing, so this is a reader who can leave, which the table assigns to posts.
  - **What it explains:** the ask belongs high, and "I do not expect you to have waited for me" works because it hands Sam the exit openly.
  - **Within the model:** the asymmetry is right; the table classifies by form where it should classify by reader. A row for the message to someone owed nothing would fix it.

- **The reader's ledger, kept off the page.** Sam reads every line against years of history the page doesn't contain: whether there were long drives, who stopped calling, whether "busyness" is true. Whether "That was my failing, not yours" lands as generous or as late depends on that history.
  - **What the model can do:** name it, as a debt raised before the first line. It cannot measure it, because the framework reads the page and this ledger is not on it.
  - **Partly outside the model:** an analysis can only flag the lines whose effect depends on it, and most lines in this letter do.
