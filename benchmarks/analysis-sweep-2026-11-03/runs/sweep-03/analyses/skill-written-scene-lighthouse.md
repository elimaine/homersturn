# Analysis: the lighthouse piece

## 1. Purpose and mindset

**Genre: a scene.** The piece is in past tense and close third person. It opens on a place and its weather, with no greeting, no headings and no claim in line one. The reader chose to stay, so the scene default of ambiguity 4 to 5 applies. It runs at **5**: the central fact (what has happened to him) is never stated, and every detail is about it.

The rules that turn with this genre:
- Withholding is allowed.
- The point goes at the bottom.
- Fragments are earned ("No car on the road. No lamps of a car.").
- Several loops can stay open.
- A refrain is welcome.

**Form: rondo in shape, villanelle in effect.** The refrain paragraphs (¶1, ¶3, ¶5) alternate with two episodes looking outward (¶2 the causeway, ¶4 the far shore and the radio). The sense of "The lamp went round" shifts with each return:
- ¶1: setting.
- ¶3: the only work her hands will do.
- ¶4 ("with the lamp going round"): the condition under which he is still only late.
- ¶5: ritual, with time going in a circle instead of forward.

**Mindset (help mode, since none was given).** I read held dread, refusing to become grief. It is far gone, about 4:
- The loss is never named.
- The sentences join on "and" where they would otherwise have to stop.
- The only short hard stops ("He was never late." "No car on the road.") are where the fear surfaces.
- No accents are spent: no dash, no swear, no punch beyond the last clause.

Three other states the piece could present, each as the last line rewritten:
- **Grief let in, 2:** "She poured the cold cup out, rinsed it under the tap, and put it back in the dresser with the good ones."
- **Fear breaking, 4:** "She poured the cold cup out and filled it and the lamp went round and she filled it again, and it would not stay warm."
- **Anger as cover, 3:** "She poured the cold cup out. Nine years, and tonight he could not manage it."

## 3. The ledger, reconstructed

| ¶ | Raises | Pays | Running debt |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The second cup (who is it for?); "One had already gone cold" (time passing, and a sign); the storm. Plants the refrain. | Nothing | 33 words, all owed |
| 2 | The road going under; "anyway" (even if he were coming, he can't now) | Who the cup is for, in part: someone who comes by car ("No car on the road") | Partial payment at about word 63; the main debt (what is keeping him) now open |

| 4 | The radio and what it would say | By inference: she knows the answer is plain and bad, and declines it. The storm, raised in ¶1, pays as escalation. | Central debt carried openly, and refused |

- **Raised and never paid on the page:** what happened to him. The reader pays it. Also "the good ones from the dresser" and "nine years," which open a faint reading (an anniversary? a return?) that nothing closes. Either it is texture or it is an accident; the skill would ask which.
- **Paid and never raised:** none. The radio is raised and refused inside one paragraph.
- **Script:** the piece is under a page, so the skill does not require it.

## 4. The seams

- **¶1→¶2: therefore.** She is waiting, so she watches the road. The link is the swing of the lamp ("whenever the light swung that way").
- **¶2→¶3: therefore.** The road is lost to her, so her hands do the one thing left. The refrain is the link.
- **¶3→¶4: and then.** The line "the way stone keeps a sound" is followed by "One by one the storm took the lights." Read generously it is a "but": her "never late" set against a world going dark. The page does not show that. This is the weakest seam. Fix: open ¶4 on the word ¶3 paid ("late").
- **¶4→¶5: therefore.** She will not hear it, so she resets the cup. Here the repeated-noun link is done exactly: "with the lamp going round" ends ¶4 and "The lamp went round" opens ¶5.

No connective fakes motion anywhere.

## 6. Topic position

The known-new chain holds throughout. "He was never late" opens on a referent the page has never named but the reader already holds (the second cup), so the chain works across two paragraphs. The one sentence a reader re-reads is a grammatical problem, not a topic problem (¶4, first sentence).

## 7. Accidental readings

- **Dangling locative:** "until the far shore was only a place she remembered being there." "There" doubles "place," and the sentence parses two ways. Fix: drop "there."
- **Modifier between two nouns:** "set it back beside the other, full," could make either cup the full one, and no refill is shown. Fix: "filled it, and set it back beside the other."
- **Renamed thing:** "the lamp" becomes "the light swung that way." Fix: "whenever the lamp came round that way," which also ties ¶2 to the refrain.
- **One word for two things:** "No lamps of a car." The skill's one-name rule would flag this. Its plain-word rule explains why to keep it: she is watching for a lamp answering hers. Keep.
- **Tense:** "for when he comes" looks like a slip by the skill's lights. It is chosen. Keep (see the last section).
- **"they paid her for"** has no antecedent. Harmless.

## 8. Unearned weight

- **None of the named tells appear.** There is no importance claim, recap, colon reveal, denied reading, pointing participle, dash or swear. The litany "No car on the road. No lamps of a car." has two items, which is the number the content has, not three by reflex.
- **The semicolon in ¶4** is not a joiner. It is the hinge between "down there" and "up here," and it earns its place.
- **One candidate: atmosphere in the stress position.** "and the stairwell kept it for her the way stone keeps a sound." It is partly earned: it plants "keep," and it turns her sentence into something carved. But it takes the end of the paragraph away from the lateness. Fix: change 2 below.

## 9. The three changes that would pay the most

1. **Close the one re-read.** "only a place she remembered being there" → "only a place she remembered being." This also keeps the better second sense: she once had a life ashore.
2. **Give ¶3's end to the change.** Change "...and the stairwell kept it for her the way stone keeps a sound" to: "He was never late. She said so to the empty stairwell, and the stairwell kept it for her the way stone keeps a sound. In nine years he had not once been late." "Late" is then planted twice, so ¶4's "only that he was late" lands as an earned refrain.
3. **Clear the last sentence's modifier.** Change "set it back beside the other, full, for when he comes" to "filled it, and set it back beside the other, for when he comes." The stress still falls on "comes."

## What the framework does not account for

1. **Narrative distance.** "for when he comes." A present-tense verb inside past narration means the narrator stops standing outside her. The last clause is her sentence, and the vigil becomes habitual: every night, from now on.
   - The skill has no concept of whose sentence a line is. The mindset dial assumes the speaker is the writer, while this piece's effect is the gap between narrator and character closing in one word.
   - **Inside the model if added:** a section on distance, saying where meaning goes (into tense and person) and that the reader is owed knowing whose voice they are in.

2. **Frequency.** "The lamp went round," told four times, stands for an event that happened hundreds of times. The same goes for "whenever the light swung that way." The repetition does the work of summary, covering time, while staying the size of a scene.
   - The skill's tempo covers duration only (scene, summary, pause). It does not cover how often.
   - **Inside the model:** add frequency to tempo.

3. **The character's ledger, and payment by the reader.** "Down there a voice would want to... say it plainly and have her say she understood." The piece declines to say it plainly to us exactly as she declines to hear it, so the reader's knowing becomes her knowing. The central debt is paid by the reader, never by the page.
   - The skill keeps one ledger, the reader's. Its machinery would count this debt as a failure: the script reports "raised, never paid," and the withheld word is "never for a thing you will not answer." Yet dial 5 ("more found than told") says this is the point.
   - **Inside the model**, but the model currently contradicts itself here. Fixable with a second ledger (what the character knows and what she lets herself know) and a `[pay: inferred]` mark.

4. **Sound.** "The lamp went round" opens ¶3, and "the way stone keeps a sound" closes it. "Round" rhymes with "sound," and the last four monosyllables slow the line, so the paragraph ends on an echo: the sentence does what the stairwell does.
   - The skill's rhythm is sentence length and where sentences break. It has nothing on phonetic texture.
   - **Outside the three axes** (where meaning goes, what the reader is owed, what the page looks like). This is what the page sounds like. The music metaphor invites it, but it would be a new axis.
   - It also means change 2 above costs something the skill cannot see: the rhyme stops closing the paragraph.

5. **Word motifs below the refrain.** "Keeping it turning was the work... the stairwell kept it... stone keeps a sound." Also "only" three times and "late" three times. These accumulate without being noticed. "Keeper" is withheld without any mark: the reader supplies the word for what she is.
   - The skill's refrain is a verbatim line planted twice, and its one-name rule sees word repetition only as an accident. It has no category for a motif.
   - **Inside the model if added:** a motif section, the refrain at the scale of a word, and the withheld word without the ——— mark.

6. **A possible failure: physical continuity.** "rinsed it under the tap." She is in the gallery and "did not go down to the radio." If the tap is below, she went down past the radio. That either undercuts ¶4 or deepens it, and the page does not say which.
   - The skill's accidental readings are all grammatical. It has no check on where bodies are.
   - **Inside the model if added:** an accidental reading opened by the world of the scene rather than by the syntax.
