# Analysis: "How the editor saves"

*Shape counted by hand; the script was not run.*

## 1. The purpose, from the evidence

This is a **help doc**, the closest row to "a repo doc, a spec, a runbook". The evidence:

- an H1 that names a feature
- an H2 subhead
- second person throughout
- imperatives ("Hold the undo button", "Click an entry")
- no greeting, scene or tense shift

The reader came for one thing. The dial is **1 to 2**. The rules that apply:

- the answer goes where the reader will look
- one name for one thing
- nothing withheld
- the end is the last useful point

The piece mostly obeys this. Its failures are of the dial-1 kind: readings it opened by accident and questions it left open.

## 3. The ledger, reconstructed

- **Title.** Raises: *how the editor saves*.
- **¶1.** Pays the title's headline: it saves for you. Raises: *when?* Debt is zero.
- **¶2.** Pays *when* with two triggers: a pause, and leaving the page. It also pays an unasked worry: "nothing you wrote is lost". That is a promise the piece now has to keep. Debt is zero.
- **¶3.** Raises without a setup. "Undoes to the last save, not the last keystroke" opens a question: *what happens to what I typed since the last save?* The answer implied by the sentence is that it is discarded. That sits against ¶2's "nothing you wrote is lost". ¶2 scoped that promise to leaving, but the reader carries the reassurance, not the scope.
- **¶4.** Pays *how to go further back* with the list of saves. Raises a further question it never pays: *if I return to an older save, what happens to the newer ones, and to the text I had?*

Raised and never paid:

- the fate of unsaved typing on undo
- the fate of newer saves on restore
- whether undo can be undone

Paid and never raised: none.

## 4. The seams

- **¶1 → ¶2: therefore.** It has a repeated-noun link: ¶1 ends "save", ¶2 opens "A save". This is clean.
- **¶2 → ¶3: and then.** ¶2 ends "on the way out" and ¶3 opens "The undo button". A new topic arrives with no tie to the old one, though the true relation is *therefore*: undo is built on the saves just described.
  - Fix: open ¶3 on "save", for example "Each save is a point you can undo to."
- **¶3 → ¶4: therefore.** One click goes back one save, so you hold the button to go further. "Undo button" carries across. This is clean.

## 5. The five measurements

**¶1**
- Band: plain, closing on a punch (land).
- Stress: "You never press save." The consequence is said plainly, which is the right use of the slot.
- Held question: opens *when*.
- Feeling: reassurance, low key.
- Planted line: none.

**¶2**
- Band: lean.
- Stress: "on the way out". This is a qualifier in the stress slot. The change is "nothing you wrote is lost", and it sits one phrase too early.
  - Fix: "so leaving never loses what you wrote."
- Held question: *when* is paid.
- Feeling: reassurance, steady.
- Planted line: none, but "nothing … is lost" acts as one, because ¶3 tests it.

**¶3**
- Band: lean.
- Stress: "when it was last written". The slot is spent on a renamed verb (see section 7).
- Held question: opens *what happens to my keystrokes*.
- Feeling: a small jolt of doubt that the piece does not answer.
- Planted line: none.

**¶4**
- Band: plain.
- Stress: "return the draft to that save". This is the last useful point, correct for the genre.
- Held question: moves *how far back can I go*, leaves *what happens to newer saves*.
- Feeling: control, low key.
- Planted line: none.

## 6. The topic position

¶3's second sentence opens on "One click", which is known enough. It ends on "last written", a new term where the reader expected the known one, "saved". This is the sentence a careful reader takes twice.

## 7. Accidental readings

**"the last save"**
- Line: "The undo button undoes to the last save, not the last keystroke."
- Problem: if you have just paused and the draft is saved, the last save is the current state. Does undo then do nothing, or go back to the one before? There are two readings and the doc means one.
- Fix: say both cases.

**A thing renamed**
- Line: "the state it was in when it was last written"
- Problem: "written" stands in for "saved", and "written" can also mean "typed". It is the doc's key term swapped at the stress position.
- Fix: "when it was last saved."

**Synonym cycle**
- Line: "list of saves / Each entry is one saved state"
- Problem: "save", "saved state", "state" and "entry" are four names for one or two things.
- Fix: "Each entry is one save."

**Referent never introduced**
- Line: "the clock resets"
- Problem: no clock was mentioned. It is small, but it is a definite article on something the reader was never given.
- Fix: "the two seconds start again."

## 8. Unearned weight

There are no importance claims, no recap, no colon reveal, no dashes and no three by reflex.

"Not the last keystroke" has the shape of a denied reading. It is earned, because the reader really does hold the keystroke model of undo. This is "concede before you are forced to" done right.
