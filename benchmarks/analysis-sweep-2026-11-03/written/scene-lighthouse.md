## Piece

The lamp went round, and the rain came at the glass sideways. She had set two cups out an hour ago, the good ones from the dresser, and poured. One had already gone cold.

From the gallery she could see the causeway whenever the light swung that way. A black line, and the sea climbing over it a hand's width at a time. No car on the road. No lamps of a car. The tide was wrong now for a car anyway, and getting wronger.

The lamp went round. Keeping it turning was the work, the one thing they paid her for, and tonight it was the only thing her hands would agree to do. He was never late. In nine years he had not once been late, and she said so to the empty stairwell, and the stairwell kept it for her the way stone keeps a sound.

One by one the storm took the lights on the far shore, until the far shore was only a place she remembered being there. She did not go down to the radio. Down there a voice would want to tell her something, would want to say it plainly and have her say she understood; and up here, with the lamp going round, it was still only that he was late.

The lamp went round. She poured the cold cup out, rinsed it under the tap, and set it back beside the other, full, for when he comes.
