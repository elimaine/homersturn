## Piece

**Name It Last**

Name the product last. Most teams name it first, in the kickoff, on day one, before anyone knows what the thing does.

A name is a promise. It tells someone what to expect before they read a word of your docs, before they click anything. You cannot make that promise honestly until you know what you are shipping, and on day one you do not.

Name it early and the name starts to steer. A feature gets called off-brand for a brand that is three weeks old. A direction gets dropped because it isn't "who we are." The word you chose when you knew the least now overrules what you learned.

And the name is sticky. By the time the product is real enough to name well, changing it means admitting the first name was wrong, so nobody does. You ship under the placeholder anyway, and the promise and the product never quite line up.

So treat the name like the title of a paper: write it when the work is done and there is something to title. Call it internal-tool-7 until then. Ugly names don't get defended, and nothing rides on them, so the product stays free to become whatever it turns out to be.

Then name it. You will know what it does, who it is for, what it is not. The naming will be easy, because the name finally has something real to describe.

Name it last.
