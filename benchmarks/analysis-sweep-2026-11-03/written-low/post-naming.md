## Piece

Name the product last.

The name is the one decision that feels like progress and isn't. You write it on the whiteboard, order the stickers, buy the domain, and none of it is the thing you're building.

You don't know the thing yet. That's not a failure of planning; it's the nature of the work. The product you ship is the one you found while building the one you specified. A name chosen in week one describes a guess. You named a map before you'd walked the ground.

Worse, it starts to steer. Once a thing is called Courier, every feature argues for carrying. The name becomes an argument you forgot you were having, and it wins quietly, because nobody reopens a decision that already has a logo.

Sure, the team needs a word to say in standup. That's what a codename is for, a handle you can drop without mourning.

Choose it when the shape stops moving. By then you can name what it does, not what you hoped it would. The word has one job. It tells a stranger, in one breath, what they get. You can only write that sentence once you can say it.

So keep the placeholder. Call it the thing, the tool, the internal codename that embarrasses you. Ship under it longer than feels comfortable. The name you pick at the end will be truer than the one you'd have defended for a year — and cheaper to live with.
