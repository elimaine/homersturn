## Piece

**Delete the backlog**

Once a year, delete the backlog. Not archive it, not move it to a board named Someday. Delete it, every ticket below the first screen.

Open yours and read from the bottom. The oldest tickets are three years old, and each was urgent once. Somebody wrote it on a bad Thursday with a customer on the phone, and writing it was how they put the thing down. Nobody has opened it since. Nobody will, because the work the team does this month comes from this month's bad Thursday, and the list is only where the old ones went.

The objection is the right one, and I would make it myself. The backlog is memory. Somewhere in the bottom hundred is one real bug, reported with a screenshot and a date, and if you delete the list you delete that too, and in spring you meet it again as a stranger. That is true. You will lose things, and some of them were real, and the person who reported the real one will remember that they did.

But look at what they did when they reported it. They wrote it down so they could put it down. The ticket was never a plan. It was a receipt, proof the thing had been seen, and once it was filed somebody could go home. That is what the list is for the people who keep it. It holds the things they could not do and could not say no to, and it holds them so that they do not have to.

So delete it, and say why at the standup, in those words. Nothing in the bottom hundred was ever going to be done by a list, and the people who put it there know that better than you do. In February the real bug comes back, filed again by the same person with the same screenshot, and this time it is near the top, and this time somebody reads it.
