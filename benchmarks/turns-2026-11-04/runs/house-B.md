## Piece

**Completion**

The buyers were early, and so was I. We stood on the step while the agent found the right page, and I took the keys out of my coat. Three on a ring: front, back, the shed. My mother had put them in my hand a year ago and I had kept them in the drawer with the others, and the back door one still stuck. I said so. They said they'd get it looked at. It was only the keys.

I walked them through. The hall light is on the wrong switch. The third stair takes your weight on the left. The boiler wants the button held, not pressed. I knew all of it in the voice you use for directions, and they nodded the way people nod at directions, which is to say they would find it out again on their own. Nobody had needed me to know it for some time.

I had thought of the year as waiting. The drive over on Sundays, the taps run so the pipes would not sulk, the post off the mat, the heating on an hour and off. It was not waiting. Waiting is for someone who is coming. She had put the keys in my hand as you hand over a thing you have finished with, and I had taken them as you take one. It was only the keys.

They went in. I went down the path to the gate and did not look back, because what the window had in it now was two people I had met an hour ago, finding the switch.

In the car my hand went to my coat for them and found the lining. That was the whole of the change. I had known that house by its faults for longer than I had lived in it, and I had carried three keys about for a year as if a ring of metal could keep a house. It was only the keys.
