# Analysis: *homersturn*, read as a repo doc

The script was not run. The glance below is measured by hand, at about 3,800 words for the whole piece.

## 1. Purpose and mindset

**Purpose: a repo doc.** The evidence is the headings, the code spans, the file paths, the tables and the numbered workflow. It has two readers:

- **The model** loads it whole. Its strength is 5 and it cannot leave, but it writes next.
- **The person** skims. Their strength is 3 and they can leave at every heading.

The binding reader is the person. The doc row asks for three things: the answer where they will look, one name for one thing, and the smallest valid structure.

**Dials I took:**

- Ambiguity 2, from the doc row.
- Strength 3.
- Mindset: an assured teacher, wry, impatient with machine prose, at about 2. It spikes to 3 at "the mark of machine prose" and "models reach for it in the same place on every page."

The intro and the figurative register ("Mana", "sforzando", "another costume") run at 3. That is one above its own row.

No mindset was asked for. It could present one of these three instead:

- **The calm master, 1.** Rules said plainly, one metaphor family (the ledger), the irritation cut. It reads as a style manual.
- **The prosecutor, 3.** Machine prose is the defendant on every page, and Homer's grave returns at the end as the verdict. This pays the opening stake.
- **The workshop note, 1.** Workflow at the top, theory pushed to `manifold-and-ledger.md`. The person who came for one thing finds it on screen one.

## 2. The glance

- **Dense opening.** "Ambiguity is the most powerful tool in good writing…" is about 128 words, roughly ten printed lines. By the skill's own measure ("four printed lines are half a phone screen") that is more than a screen. It is longer than ¶2 and ¶3 combined. → End ¶1 on "This skill is the placement." and give the name its own short paragraph.
- **Spread.** Blocks run from 5 words ("Pick one; never stack them.") to about 185.
- **Longest block, and the next wall.** The mindset bullet is about 185 words, and the strength bullet next to it is about 165. Both sit in the first section after the intro, so the reader who survives the opening meets two two-screen walls in a row. → Split each (see §8).
- **Repeated frames:**
  - "in another costume", "in other clothes", "participial clothes", "wear its clothes": four uses.
  - "at the scale of the page / the cast / the piece" plus "by figure": four.
  - Bold lead-in plus colon: about thirty.
  - An aphorism closing a section: seven.
  - Parentheses: over twenty, against "once a page."
  - Semicolons: about thirty, against "the semicolon… the same mark in other clothes."

## 4. The seams

- **And then, between sections.** The order runs Glance → Positions → Join → Purpose. Positions ¶2 opens on "The reader who can leave" and the Purpose heading is "the reader who can leave." The Join section sits between them and breaks the repeated-noun join. → Move Join to follow The model.
- **And then, inside The model.** Manifold → ledger → mindset → rhythm → tempo have no joins. One honest join exists: "Tempo is the frame" → "Paragraph length is tempo made visible" (the repeated noun).
- **But, honest.** "Equal paragraphs hum, but while reading, not at a glance."
- **Renamed join.** "it turns by therefore or but, and a jump with no cause is the seam failure in another costume." The Join section never says "seam." → "the join failure", or put "seam" in the Join heading.
- **Faked connective.** "pay the trust back with interest, so twists are rare and late ones larger and fewer." The "so" claims a consequence the clause does not deliver.

## 7. Accidental readings, unearned weight, humming, the narrator

**Accidental readings:**

- **"frame" carries three senses.** "Tempo is the frame." / "a frame used twice" / "outside the frame." → "Tempo is the clock"; keep "frame" for the rhetorical one.
- **"model" carries two senses in one section.** "## The model" holds "A model's default state is helpful." → Rename the heading "How meaning works."
- **"topic" carries two senses in one paragraph.** The defined "topic position" (what the reader holds) sits beside "a page where every paragraph opens on its topic sentence" (the school sense). → "opens on its point."
- **"Never both as a template":** both what? Top and bottom together, or each? → "Neither, as a template."
- **"is a tell."** This contradicts Analyze's "readers cannot tell and neither can you." → "reads as a template."
- **"a name or a number fills it":** trust or pool? "Fills" was the pool's verb. → Name the noun.
- **"Bold is a punch."** The defined punch is a last line, so this gives "punch" a second sense. → "Bold is emphasis."
- **"start with the abstract":** a summary, or the abstract idea? → "start with the claim."
- **"Height, hair and a job title raise and pay nothing":** raise something, or raise nothing? → "raise nothing and pay nothing."
- **"A borrowed phrase carries the character and the narrator at once."** This sits next to "a cliché pays nothing" and seems to contradict it. → "A cliché in a character's mouth…"
- **"Outside this skill: … the reader's time"** contradicts the Purpose section. → "what the reader already knows."

**One thing, several names:**

- "pool" / "strength" / "capacity" / "Mana" → Use "pool."
- "the hum" / "uniform density" / "the same density" → Use "the same density."
- "promise" / "raise" → Use "raise."
- "join" / "seam" → Pick one.

**Definitions after use.** "pool" (¶2), "refrain" and "the skill's own rule" (¶3), "the ledger as written here" (Dials), "a punch" and "the seam failure" (Model).

**Used and never defined.** "lean", "keyed", "feeling". Workflow says "four words" but the template has five slots: "scene · staccato · restate · plain · no punch."

**Gifts:**

- "Homer turning in his grave": the cliché is paid by the pun.
- "the postscript is what is said after the ledger closes, outside the frame" is paid, structurally, by the final "Outside this skill:" paragraph.
- "Prose to be heard keeps its repetitions" echoes the bard at the fire.
- "The title is the first raise": the doc's title raises, and ¶1 pays it.

**Unearned weight:**

- "the most powerful tool" (an importance claim).
- "The last one is the one to earn your way out of." (a kicker, unpaid).
- "the commonest thing that makes good prose hard to parse" (a superlative, unpaid).
- "and never lies." (a kicker).
- "is the ledger used as a tool." (a kicker).
- The Purpose coda "Withholding the reveal is for readers who stay" restates Positions ¶2, and Do not restates it a third time with no shift. That is a clarification nobody needed.

**The figure humming.** The frames listed in §2.

- The bold lead-ins break the doc's own rule, "Bold is a punch; on a keyword it pays nothing."
- Every bullet opening on its point is the template Positions forbids. That template is correct for a doc, so the rule needs a doc exception.

**The dash that joins.** There are no em dashes. The semicolon takes their place about thirty times, for example "Pick one; never stack them." (→ two sentences) and "Between any two beats the word is 'therefore' or 'but'; if 'and then' fits…"

**Lines for the narrator:**

- "Rules other guides give as taste, kept because the model explains them." This is the named fault exactly: what others leave out and the teller keeps in. → "Each rule's reason is in `references/sentence.md`."
- "what the machines did with his trade before this." Here "before this" credits the skill. → Cut "before this."

## 8. The three changes that would pay the most

1. **The opening.**
   - Line: "Ambiguity is the most powerful tool in good writing and the most abused one in machine writing."
   - Change: Cut the superlative and open on "A flat model says ambiguous things everywhere, at the same density, and the reader stops hearing any of it." End ¶1 on "This skill is the placement." Move the name's three readings to a short ¶ of their own.
   - Why it pays most: the opening's debt becomes only the second sentence, the stress lands on what the skill is, and the skill's own bench says this is worth a point of five.
2. **The second wall.**
   - Line: the mindset bullet, "The opinion is on the matter, never on the telling…", and the strength bullet, "trust times what is due…"
   - Change: Move the narrator rule to its own bullet, give trust its own sentence or bullet, and fix "which rhythm carries."
   - Why: each bullet then carries one thing, and the page stops humming at two screens.
3. **One name per thing, planted as the refrain.**
   - Line: "uniform density is what a mind with no state sounds like" / "Mana regenerates."
   - Change: Say "the same density" everywhere "uniform density" and "the hum" now stand, use "pool" for "Mana," and keep "frame" to one sense.
   - Why: the opening's phrase is then planted twice before "Do not write every paragraph at the same density" returns it, now as a rule rather than a diagnosis. The conclusion pays the opening, and the naming row is kept.

## Where the shape could teach the rule

None of these would be noticed by a reader who came for a rule:

- **Headings that state.** Only "The join: therefore, but, never and then" obeys "A subhead states something or raises nothing." Headings such as "The glance: never open on a wall" and "The entrance: the line an actor reads them from" would make the table of contents a checklist for the person who can leave, and the Do not recap could shrink.
- **Definition before use, and the repeated-noun join.** Move Join to follow The model. "Seam" is then defined beside its first use, and Positions hands "the reader who can leave" straight to Purpose.
- **The refrain.** "At the same density" planted in ¶1, again in The model, and returned in Do not. This is change 3 above.
- **The postscript.** This already works. Leave it unannotated.
- **The stress position of the doc.** Move "Do not invent a claim…" up into the brief so the recap is only a recap. The last useful point stays last: "whether a claim is true."

## What the framework does not account for

1. **The reader who writes next.**
   - Line: "the semicolon, the comma-and and the participial tail are the same mark in other clothes." The line is itself built on a hummed frame, inside a doc with about thirty semicolons and about thirty bold-colon lead-ins.
   - Effect: a model that loads this page imitates its register, so the doc's style is likely to recur in its output. That matters more than any single rule it states.
   - Fit: ¶3 guards against reusing quoted lines, but not against borrowing the register. The model could explain this with a new Purpose row: "a prompt or a skill: the reader writes next; it is owed the register it should produce." This is inside the model, under what the reader is owed.
2. **The rule built to be carried away.**
   - Lines: "Felt below shown is tension; shown above felt is melodrama." and "Two characters at one state in one rhythm are one character with two names."
   - Effect: the symmetry makes each rule portable. The reader quotes it from memory later, which a doc reader is owed. The skill's dash rule condemns the first one's semicolon, and its aphorism rule targets the second.
   - Fit: the ledger only counts payment inside the page, not what leaves with the reader. This is half inside the model (a section on what is owed at the reader's next use) and half outside it (the chiasmus is sound, which the skill sends to Poet).
3. **A rule broken on its own page.**
   - Line: "Bold is a punch; on a keyword it pays nothing," inside a page of bold keywords. Likewise "The parenthesis is the aside under the breath, once a page."
   - Effect: each visible self-violation discounts every other "never." A model reader may learn that the rules are decorative.
   - Fit: the trust mechanism could explain it ("unpaid promises lower it") if a section said that a rule is a promise the page giving it must keep first. Inside the model.
4. **The table as a reading path.**
   - Lines: the Purpose and Forms tables.
   - Effect: these are the most usable blocks for the person who can leave. They look up by row, not by reading in order.
   - Fit: the glance names paragraphs, lists and subheads, never tables or lookup. "What the page looks like" could hold it with one bullet. Inside the model.
