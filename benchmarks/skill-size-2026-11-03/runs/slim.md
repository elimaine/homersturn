## Piece

The sword lay in the reeds where the flood had dropped it, bright and too long for the mouse by half. She had come down to the water for water, nothing more. She stood over it until the river forgot her and ran on.

Upriver the kingdom was going quiet the way a field goes quiet. Velvet Grael had taken the winter grain into his storehouses and set a clerk at the door. He did not shout and he did not steal, he said; he counted. He weighed each family's share against its usefulness and found most families wanting. Children learned the sound of his soft feet on the boards overhead, and learned to be smaller. He had never once raised his voice. That was the worst of him.

The mouse was no one. She mended nets by the shallows. She had had a brother once, weighed, found wanting, and buried. She looked at the sword again. The sword was still too long.

She was afraid in the ordinary way, the way that makes the hands cold. She did not feel brave and did not become brave. She only decided that the sword being too long was not a reason. That was the whole of it.

So she took it by the hilt that was made for a wider hand, and she dragged it. She dragged it up the bank. She dragged it through the frost, and the point drew a long line behind her all the way to the storehouse door, as if she were underlining something.

Grael was counting when she came in. He looked at her, and at the blade she could barely lift, and he smiled, because he could add. One mouse. One sword she had to drag. He opened his soft mouth to name her share.

She could not swing it. She could fall. She fell with all of herself behind the point, and the point did what she could not. The blade had always been too long to swing. It was exactly long enough to fall on. Grael made a sound then that no one had ever heard him make. It was not soft.

They had followed the line in the frost without meaning to, the whole village, net-menders and the hungry and the found-wanting, and they filled the doorway as the clerk's ledger hit the floor and its numbers scattered like mice across the boards.

The grain went home that winter in small arms.
