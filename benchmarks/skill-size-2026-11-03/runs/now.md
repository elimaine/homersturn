## Piece

The sword lay in the long grass, taller than the mouse who found it, and heavier than anything true should be. Someone had dropped it there who did not need it anymore.

You have to understand what the kingdom was by then. The chinchilla had come down from the high rocks in a coat of grey smoke, the softest thing anyone in the valley had ever touched, and every creature that touched it loved it, and that was the whole trouble. It took the autumn seed and called it a tithe. It took the lame vole's burrow and called it good order. And when the field mice had learned to live on what was left, it took that too, and it smiled the whole time, because the soft are never the ones who have to be cruel out loud.

The mouse was one of those field mice. It had been told, its whole life, by mice, that it was too small to matter.

The sword did not care how small the mouse was. It cared only whether the mouse would lift it, and for a long time the mouse could not. It dragged the thing through the mud by the hilt and slept in its shadow and woke with both paws raw, and on the ninth morning it held the blade clear of the ground, shaking, for the length of one breath.

So the mouse went up the high road to the soft court, and the guards did not even draw, because why would you draw on something too small to matter. The chinchilla drew nothing either. But the chinchilla had seen the sword, and it did the thing soft power always does when the edge arrives: it offered. A cushion. A title. A full share of the autumn seed. It offered, in that voice like fur, to make the mouse the second softest thing in the kingdom.

And the mouse, who was too small to matter, lifted the sword.

It was not much of a fight. Soft things fear a hard edge more than anything in the world, and the chinchilla, for all its court and all its coat, had never once had to be brave. They tell it to the small ones now, down in the burrows, the way you tell a child a thing you need them to keep: that the sword was heavier than anything true should be, and that someone lifted it anyway, and that someone turned out to be exactly the right size.
