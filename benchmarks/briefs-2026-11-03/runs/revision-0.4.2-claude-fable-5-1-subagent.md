## Piece

The chinchilla came to the barn in a cage with the door open and never went back in it. He went up the ladder to the loft instead, all of him, and the loft has been his since. He is the size of a cat and the shape of a loaf and he wears a ring of fence wire on his head, and we called him the Soft. That was a joke.

The joke lasted a year. Every night a tenth of the grain went up the ladder on our backs, and the night Tobin's family kept theirs, the Soft came to the edge of the loft and gnawed, slowly, through the beam the nursery hung from, and let the floor do the rest. He did not come down for it. He has never come down for anything.

Ada found the sword under the loft floor, in the gap where the boards had shrunk, which is the one place in the barn he cannot see because it is under him. It was a sword and not a pin, since a pin has no edge and this had two, and it was the length of her, which meant it had been made for one of us. She did not wave it. She turned it to the light and looked at the edge for a while and said, "Somebody made this for a mouse. So somebody thought a mouse would need it."

We tried to say the things you say. He is too big. He is fat and slow and he sleeps all day and you will be under the floor by morning. She listened to all of it and climbed the ladder anyway, and watching her go up I understood what we had been laughing at for a year: he was soft, we had meant, because he was weak, and he was soft, I saw then, because nothing had ever got through to him. Not the owl. Not us. Everything he ever wanted had been carried up to him. Somebody had thought a mouse would need it.

He did not get up. He never gets up. She went in under the fur where there is more of him than there is anything in him, and for a long time nothing happened. Then he screamed. We had not known he had a voice. He stood, which we had not known either, and the crown of wire fell off, and the Soft came down the ladder faster than a thing that size should move, and across the floor, and out through the gap under the door into the yard, and the barn held still until the dogs started.

Ada came down the ladder with the sword and nothing on it but dust. She did not keep it. She put it back under the loft floor in the gap where the boards had shrunk, and when Tobin asked her why, she said, "Somebody will need it."
