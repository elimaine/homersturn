## Piece

The lock is older than your picks. You kneel to it. Through the boards above you, the innkeeper snores. First pin. Second. The third binds.

You hold the tension and wait, and the snore holds too, so you take the rhythm from him: lift on the in-breath, set on the out. The pin drops. The bolt turns with a sound like a knuckle cracking. Your pick comes out bent at the tip, and you have one straight one left. Above you, the breath goes on in and out.

Nine steps down, and the cold comes up to meet you by the fourth. Barrels, a smell of vinegar, a lamp with oil still in it. The ledger is not hidden. It lies on the end barrel under a cloth, the way a man leaves a thing when nobody has ever got this far. You open it to the first page. Names down the left, sums beside them, and beside each sum a second, smaller sum in a different ink. His own name is nowhere in it.

The stair behind you goes back up to the door you left open, and past it to the taproom, and that is the only way out. Above you, the snoring stops.
