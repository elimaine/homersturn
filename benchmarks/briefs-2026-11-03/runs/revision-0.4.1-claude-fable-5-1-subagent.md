## Piece

The sword was in the ditch below the mill, and Pim found it the morning the chinchilla took the flour. It was longer than she was. She looked at it for a while, and then she said the only thing anyone remembers her saying about it. "Well," said Pim. "It will have to be dragged."

It is hard to hate a chinchilla. He came in the autumn with eleven rats and asked for the tithe the way one asks for the salt, and he never once raised his voice, and he is mostly fur, and the fur is mostly air, and a creature built like that can hardly be blamed for— no. He rolled in it. He went into the granary where the kingdom's flour for the winter was heaped to the rafters and he rolled in it, for his coat, the way his kind do in dust, and he came out white and pleased, and it was not food after that, and he knew it.

She dragged it up out of the ditch and across the mill yard, and the sound a sword makes on stones is a sound every rat in the kingdom knows from the traps, and by the time she reached the granary ramp all eleven were up on the wall, looking down, and not one came down. "You'll never lift that," said one. "No," said Pim. "It will have to be dragged."

The chinchilla was in the flour. He had it up to his ears and was turning in it, slowly, with his eyes shut, and when the point of the sword came scraping in over the sill he opened one eye and did not get up. "Mind the flour," said the chinchilla. "I have only just got it even."

She could not lift it, and she did not try. She set the pommel against the sill and the point at the height of her own head, which was the height of his belly, and she held it there. He came. He came because she was small and nothing had ever refused him, and he came fast, and the point went in to the hilt and met nothing, and then the fur let go of him, all at once, the way a chinchilla's fur will let go when something has hold of it, and he went backward out of it, and what hung on the sword was a grey cloud the size of a chinchilla, and what sat on the floor was not.

The rats came down off the wall then, all eleven, and looked at what sat on the floor, which was a grey animal about two mice long, white to the ears, and shaking, and then they looked at the sword, which was the largest thing in the room. "My coat," said the chinchilla. Pim leaned on the hilt. "Well," said Pim. "He will have to be dragged."
